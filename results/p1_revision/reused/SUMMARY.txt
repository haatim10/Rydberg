Paper 1 reviewer revision: reuse and practical-rank diagnostic

This analysis preserves every original experiment file. The two evidence
sources below remain separate; no extra fresh Monte Carlo worlds were generated.

1. Classical RSR results recovered from the audited Paper 2 campaign

Only classical EM-GS and HS-GS errors are used. These are not ML gains.
Settings match the Paper 1 principal experiment: N=32, K=3, P=30, aggregate
SNR=5 dB, independent per-user 3--7 paths, 100 final updates, 25 rank-candidate
updates, four Cadzow sweeps, held-out fraction 0.3, and final refit on all pilots.
Each RSR condition has 400 paired worlds. These worlds are distinct from the
original 27,300-trial Paper 1 verification campaign.

At single-user RSR=0,5,10,12,15,20,25 dB, HS-GS ratio-of-sums gains over
EM-GS are 1.827,2.512,2.261,2.198,2.339,2.445,2.335 dB. All pointwise 95%
paired-bootstrap intervals are positive. Both estimators lose accuracy at
the weakest reference. Structural benefit persists at this array size and
SNR, but it is not monotonic with RSR. The current study does not establish
RSR sensitivity at every SNR or array size.

Assumed noise variance was also offset by -3,0,+3 dB on the same 400 reference
worlds. EM-GS NMSE is -10.688,-10.728,-10.723 dB; HS-GS NMSE is
-12.895,-12.926,-12.939 dB. This is noise-variance specification sensitivity,
not correlated-noise, array-calibration or near-field robustness.

The reproducible JSON and CSV contain absolute NMSE, ratio-of-sums gain,
paired-median gain, pointwise intervals from 4,000 paired bootstrap resamples,
rank-fallback fraction, exact settings and input/source SHA-256 hashes.
Bootstrap seed: 20261007. Figure pairs: rsr_classical and
noise_variance_classical, each PDF and PNG.

2. Receiver-available effective-rank diagnostic from unstructured EM-GS

We deterministically replayed only EM-GS on 10,400 existing Paper 1 worlds:
18 array/SNR cells (N=8,16,32; P=30; six SNRs; 400 each) plus the eight
matched N32/P20/random-SNR complexity cells (400 each). No HS-GS rank search
was repeated. All 10,400 reconstructed EM errors exactly equal their saved
original values in this environment. Replay took 84.4 seconds after the
preflight. True effective ranks and channel energies also agree with the
original saved values. Seven automated reuse tests pass.

Per-user EM estimates, receiver-available effective-rank proxies, true ranks,
path counts, selected ranks, old EM/HS errors, trial indices and replay errors
are saved in proxy/*.npz; adjacent JSON records give input/output hashes and
source/seed provenance. Truth is used only for evaluation, not estimation.

The proxy becomes more informative as SNR increases, but is biased upward
by estimation noise. For N32/P30, correlation with the true trial-mean rho
increases from 0.202 at -5 dB to 0.914 at 20 dB. At 5 dB the mean proxy is
0.577 while the true mean is 0.244, and the actual HS-GS median improvement
is 2.544 dB. Thus the true-channel rho~0.5 heuristic cannot simply be used
as a threshold on this noisy receiver proxy.

Within fixed, predeclared 5-dB SNR bands of the matched complexity study,
proxy-versus-gain Spearman correlation ranges from -0.098 in [-10,-5) dB
to -0.797 in [15,20] dB. True-rho and normalized true-path-count correlations
are also reported as privileged diagnostics. No predictor or threshold was
fitted to these data. Pointwise 95% percentile intervals now use 4,000
paired whole-trial bootstrap resamples, seed 20261007. The resampling plan
was specified after the initial descriptive point estimates were inspected;
it does not retrospectively make this analysis prespecified in the original
simulation campaign. All users are retained through the trial-level means.
The N32/P30 proxy-to-truth intervals are [0.100,0.297] at -5 dB and
[0.890,0.931] at 20 dB. The matched-complexity proxy-to-gain intervals are
[-0.183,-0.012] in the lowest band and [-0.836,-0.752] in the highest.
SNR bands only partially control SNR. Final HS-GS
effective rank was intentionally excluded to avoid a correlation imposed
by the structural processing itself.
