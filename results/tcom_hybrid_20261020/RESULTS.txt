HYBRID ESTIMATOR: FRESH PAIRED COMPARISON

Against the frozen latest estimator, the whole-grid comparison supports higher error for the hybrid in NMSE and does not resolve a difference at the adjusted confidence level in BER.
Against compact sparse, the whole-grid comparison supports higher error for the hybrid in NMSE and supports higher error for the hybrid in BER.
The confirmed policy selected zero additional EM everywhere. The hybrid and its zero-tail control are therefore the same estimator on this grid; this is not evidence that additional EM cannot help.
The confirmed hybrid does not meet the intended accuracy objective and these results do not support replacing the previous estimator.

The hybrid start and additional EM count are frozen from observable development criteria and runtime eligibility before this fresh test. The no-EM arm uses exactly the same chosen start and final filter with zero additional EM. It isolates the continuation; it is not the raw pre-filter starting estimate.
Both starts already execute ten EM/structure stages. Zero tail means no ADDITIONAL EM updates. The global schedule contains 30 globally trained parameters; the adaptive controller contains 188 parameters and conditions its decisions on observations. The fixed schedule still uses normalization and known noise variance.
Primary claims concern hybrid versus latest and compact over all 153 conditions. The two contrasts across NMSE and BER form four outcomes with Bonferroni adjustment. Other methods, subgroups, raw starts and no-EM comparisons are descriptive.
This report compares the fixed receivers; it does not automatically select, deploy or recommend replacing an estimator.

INTERPRETATION AND VERIFICATION
The primary NMSE is an equal-cell mean of dB error-to-energy ratios. The pooled linear ratio is computed separately for each fit and then averaged arithmetically across fits; its dB value is the log of that mean. It weights errors and channel energy differently from the primary mean-cell dB measure. The grid has 108 of 153 conditions above 20 dB although the inherited training ends at 20 dB; neither weighting is a deployment-weighted guarantee.
There are 128 channels per reference/path setting, 1,152 physical anchors reused across 17 SNR values, and 384 local-index clusters for the 20,000 paired bootstrap draws. Indices are shared across reference, SNR, estimator and training fit within each path regime. Uncertainty is conditional on the fixed weights, selected policy and grid.
All methods use the same independent magnitude-domain data: each channel transmits all 64 joint unit-power Gray-QPSK vectors once. The detector enumerates those 64 hypotheses using the exact Rician likelihood. N=32, K=3 and P=30; reference and noise variance are known.
Zero observed errors and collapsed empirical intervals do not prove zero population BER, equivalence or population noninferiority. Bit counts are not independent channel trials.
The additional EM count is a finite continuation, not a guarantee of a converged optimum. The learned global schedule lacks an observation-conditioned controller but retains scale normalization. These comparisons do not cover every classical estimator.
Routing still has boundaries: the start changes above 30 dB; the nearest-bin strong-reference filter changes at 31.25 dB. In the separate exploratory B-proposal arm, a ten-update tail also starts across that strong-reference boundary, and the weak-reference tail changes from zero to 40 updates across 38.75 dB. These B tails are absent from the confirmed zero-tail hybrid. The earlier strong-reference filter-off jump around 40 dB remains repaired: c=4, beta=1 filtering continues from 37.5 dB upward. This gridded test does not prove continuity between sampled conditions or robustness to variance mismatch.
Selection, prediction, raw/final summary, timing and BER audits all passed. The report does not edit the paper or make a publication or novelty claim.
