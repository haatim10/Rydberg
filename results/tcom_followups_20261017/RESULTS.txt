Three completed follow-up experiments for the merged TCOM draft

The new sparse baseline, common-detector BER experiment, and controlled high-SNR sweep are complete. Existing model checkpoints and manuscript sources were left unchanged.

The ordinary comparison uses 960 new channel worlds over 15 conditions: SNR 0,5,10,15,20 dB; RSR 12 dB; two paths, four paths, or random 3–7 paths per user. Each channel is held fixed for 256 new QPSK data vectors. The detector enumerates all 64 possible three-user symbol vectors with the exact referenced-magnitude Rician likelihood. All estimators receive identical pilots and data magnitudes.

The sparse comparator is slightly more accurate on this balanced test grid and has lower aggregate BER than the learned estimator. The difference is not uniform: sparse fitting generally wins at lower SNR, while the learned estimator becomes competitive at higher SNR and wins clearly for two-path channels at 15–20 dB. The two-path aggregate NMSE difference has a confidence interval that includes zero. The learned estimator still improves BER over the existing frozen OptShrink and EM100 controls. This supports an accuracy-versus-computation comparison, with communication performance measured explicitly, rather than a claim of universal accuracy superiority.
Measured sparse/learned mean inference-time ratio: 84.03. This applies to the implemented solver and selected budgets, not an intrinsic complexity lower bound; a more optimized or shorter-budget sparse implementation may narrow the gap.

Paired learned-minus-sparse mean NMSE difference: 0.341 dB; 95% channel-bootstrap interval [0.282, 0.396]. Negative favors learned.
Paired learned-minus-em100 BER difference: -0.7666 percentage points; 95% interval [-0.8177, -0.7174]. Negative favors learned.
Paired learned-minus-optshrink BER difference: -0.0566 percentage points; 95% interval [-0.0777, -0.0373]. Negative favors learned.
Paired learned-minus-sparse_angle BER difference: 0.0848 percentage points; 95% interval [0.0672, 0.1042]. Negative favors learned.

Sparse-estimator design: a 128-column angle dictionary, continuous off-grid generated paths, exact Rician posterior-field updates, and a complex L1-penalized M-step. Regularization and iteration budget were selected using 21 fitting and 9 heldout pilots on a separate 180-world development set. True channel and path count were unavailable to selection. This is a validated first sparse implementation; it does not establish the strongest possible sparse or off-grid competitor.

The high-SNR study contains 96 evaluated conditions from 576 distinct anchor worlds. It holds channels, pilots, reference, and standardized noise fixed across SNR. Same-weight filter-off controls retain the 10 learned step sizes. EM10 and EM100 separate filtering bias from iteration limits.

These are descriptive mean crossing brackets, not certified safe cutoffs. Reference strength and path regime change the boundary; pointwise intervals are in high_snr/summary.json. Classical OptShrink settings above 20 dB are clamped to their existing 20 dB validation choice and are not optimized for the stress regime.

Uncertainty and scope: channel-block bootstrap preserves dependence between all estimators and the three fixed learned fits. It does not measure variation across new training/development populations. Low or zero error counts at high SNR need more independent channels; a degenerate zero bootstrap interval is not evidence of zero population BER. Perfect CSI is an ideal-information reference under vector ML, not a claimed mathematical lower bound on bit error. No coded throughput, hardware, or universal-channel claim is made.

Validation: sparse finite-difference gradient, exact zero-penalty EM equivalence, monotone penalized likelihood, all 64 noiseless QPSK hypotheses, independent SciPy Rician-density comparison, high-SNR endpoint replays, and cross-study byte-identical observations/estimates all passed. Original input hashes are unchanged. The three studies share data; there are 1344 distinct primary evaluation pilot worlds, excluding development and timing worlds.

For the manuscript: include this sparse method and the common-detector BER figure, explain the low/high-SNR crossover, and state the operating range explicitly. A useful next comparison is a sparse accuracy-versus-runtime curve at similar inference budgets. A useful method improvement to investigate is a filter-bypass safeguard selected on fresh validation data. The observed test crossing must not be presented as an independently validated deployment threshold.
