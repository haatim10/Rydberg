WEAK-REFERENCE TRANSITION REPAIR: FRESH NMSE, BER AND RUNTIME COMPARISON
Completed experiment. Manuscript and all earlier study outputs remain unchanged.

KEY FINDINGS
In the predeclared weak-reference transition target, repair changes mean NMSE by +16.208 dB relative to the previous bypass and +14.605 dB relative to the retrained model (positive is improvement).
Observed target BER: previous bypass 0.012772%, repair 0.000000%. The adjusted intervals below determine whether the differences are resolved by this finite sample.
4/4 prespecified primary NMSE/BER comparisons have strictly positive family-adjusted improvement intervals. This inference is conditional on the fixed models, policies and test grid.
Target cells with worse mean NMSE: 0 versus previous bypass, 0 versus retrained model. All three path-regime target results and every adverse cell follow.
Across the full grid, 10 conditions have worse mean NMSE than the old bypass, including 4 with pointwise 95% intervals supporting harm. The worst observed loss is 2.808 dB. The repair is not uniformly better than that policy.
The rule retains the original estimate in 90/153 conditions and changes it in 63. Comparing against the original therefore includes many exact identities.
Overall observed BER remains lower for the matched sparse/EM family (0.479718%) than for the repair (0.506578%); the repair preserves the original estimator's low-SNR behavior.
Primary-window p90 latency is 11.258 ms for repair versus 5.326 ms for the old bypass (2.11 times as long). This target-window cost must not be hidden by the overall median.
Final all-grid p90 latency: repair 10.957 ms; matched sparse/EM family 5.567 ms. Original outputs remain exactly unchanged through nominal 20 dB.

DESIGN AND INTERPRETATION
The three existing 30-pilot retrained checkpoints remain frozen. At nominal SNR<=20 dB the original estimator is returned exactly. Above 20 dB a development-selected lookup uses supplied noise variance and known reference power to choose the original estimator, 5/20/100 exact EM steps initialized by its output, or spectral-initialized EM100.
Every deployed decision uses receiver-observable quantities. True channels and path counts are absent from inference. A path regime is used only as a calibration stratum and a test reporting label.
The routing proxy is 10 log10(K/sigma^2), with K=3 and the declared unit-user-power normalization. Nearest declared SNR/reference bins determine the lookup; this is not an estimate of realized channel SNR.
Initialization is the hypothesis under test: retaining the learned channel as the EM starting point may avoid the poor solutions reached by the previous fresh spectral fallback. Longer continuation is not assumed universally better.
The tested repair combines learned initialization and guarded routing. This comparison does not isolate their separate causal contributions, and warm100 and spectral100 have different initialization costs.
An exploratory screen used only an already available DEVELOPMENT observation subset to define this small menu. New calibration and new test banks are independent of that screen and the exposed previous test bank.
Fresh development uses 96 channel/reference anchors per pair, with 30 fitting pilots and 9 held-out pilots. Each nonidentity candidate must have a one-sided 95% paired-bootstrap upper bound on held-out NLL change<=0 in EACH of three path regimes. Original is always admissible; constraints are never relaxed. Eligible choices minimize mean held-out NLL, using the frozen preference order for ties.
This is a conservative development screen, not a guarantee of NMSE/BER safety. Multiple selection decisions, finite data and metric mismatch remain. No test outcomes change the rule.
The primary target is reference 5 dB and SNR 32.5–45 dB: 18 conditions spanning 2, 4, and random3–7 paths. Two comparisons, repair versus previous bypass and versus retrained model, times NMSE and BER form four primary outcomes.
Separate path-regime target scores, every adverse condition, tails and all-grid results are reported so a pooled improvement cannot hide the original weakness.
Testing uses 128 fresh channel/reference anchors per pair: 1,152 unique channel/reference draws, reused at 17 SNRs to give 19,584 operating cases. Within each path regime, shared local indices preserve coupling across reference/SNR/method/fit in 20,000 bootstrap resamples.
Three-fit groups average individual estimator metrics; they are not ensembles and do not triple independent channels. NMSE is equal-cell mean dB ratio-of-sums. Pooled NMSE is shown separately because aggregation can change rankings.
BER uses every 64-vector joint GrayQPSK symbol once per channel, fresh data noise shared across estimators, and the same exact 64-hypothesis magnitude-domain Rician detector. There are 7,520,256 bits per estimator/fit across the full grid and 884,736 in the primary target. Zero observed errors do not prove zero BER.

RUNTIME AND BASELINE FAIRNESS
The new repair rule is selected before isolated timing calibration. The compact sparse cap is the measured p90 of that actual rule; eligible sparse configurations are then selected on the same fresh development observations before any tests.
That cap pools all 153 conditions and three fixed fits. It is neither a per-condition latency match nor a worst-branch runtime guarantee.
All 16 declared compact candidates passed calibration eligibility. The compact menu is a limited family, not a global sparse-optimization claim. The previous compact sparse table, full sparse and OptShrink remain frozen transfer controls. Their earlier lookup calibration is not repeated for the new study.
Some strong-reference warm100/spectral100 development losses differ by only about 1e-8 to 1e-6. These are near-equivalent finite-development choices, well above the measured arithmetic discrepancy but not evidence of a meaningful deployment advantage. The rule is not optimized for minimum runtime within a practical-equivalence tolerance.
The fresh matched family selects exact zero-penalty EM100 in 27/51 lookup bins, including 6/6 weak-reference target bins. In those cells its prediction equals direct EM100; it is a selected sparse/EM family, not an independent sparse solution.
Fresh final timing runs separately after accuracy work, with random method order and 3 repetitions per condition. All raw calls and outliers are retained. Calibration caps are measured quantiles, not hard deadlines; final cap violations are reported without reselection.
The primary-window repair p90 is +2.53% relative to the pooled calibration cap. 11/51 repair condition-specific p90 values exceed that pooled cap; the matched sparse/EM family has 0/51. Each condition pools paths and fixed fits. These values show why pooled eligibility is not a per-condition runtime guarantee.
