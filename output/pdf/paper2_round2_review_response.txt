RESPONSE TO THE LATEST PAPER 2 REVIEW
Abdullah Haatim | 8 October 2026

Scope of this revision
All seven author questions have now received an experimental answer or
an explicit, bounded explanation. Answered does not mean every weakness
has been solved. The new checks reveal important negative results. The
original selected estimator, data and results have not been overwritten,
and no new architecture was chosen using the new test scores.

Most important change to the claims
The compact estimator is not generally more accurate or much faster than
a strong classical shrinkage baseline. In a controlled fresh comparison,
in-loop OptShrink has 0.143 dB lower average per-SNR dB error, with similar
processing time. The learned model has fewer severe errors in that tested
mixture. The abstract, main results, discussion and conclusion now state
this trade-off. The earlier roughly 64-fold speed ratio is explicitly
restricted to the original rank-search HS-GS implementation, not OptShrink.

1. Put a data-driven shrinker inside the Hankel loop
Location: main paper, "A Stronger In-Loop Shrinkage Comparison";
supplement, "Controlled In-Loop OptShrink Comparison."

We added Rician EM updates followed by in-loop OptShrink and antidiagonal
averaging. Rank, mixing and iteration budget were selected using withheld
Rician likelihood on 224 development worlds, separately by known noise
level. The frozen learned models and classical methods then used identical
observations on 1,152 new test worlds. A final-only OptShrink control was
included. No true channel was used to select classical settings.

Across seven primary SNRs, NMSE is -14.319 dB for learned, -14.462 dB for
in-loop OptShrink, -14.374 dB for final-only OptShrink, and -12.890 dB for
Rician-ranked HS-GS. The learned loss against in-loop OptShrink is 0.143 dB
with conditional paired 95% interval [0.086, 0.198] dB. Severe-error rates
are 0.89% and 5.02%, respectively; the primary severe errors occur at
-10 dB SNR. Mean per-case linear error gives a different ranking, which
is reported without replacing the declared average-dB measure.

Mean full inference times are 5.67 ms for learned and 6.20 ms for in-loop
OptShrink in the same timing batch, including initialization and setting
lookup. Longer OptShrink runs can also beat learned at weak references.
This is a controlled OptShrink-based Rician reconstruction, not a full
reproduction of published LRHD. Its denoising guarantees do not carry over
automatically to correlated Hankel errors after nonlinear inversion.

2. Train different stage counts and controller widths
Location: main discussion; supplement, "Separately Trained Depth, Width
and Training Controls," including both tables and the new figure.

We completed 25 matched training jobs: seven variants with three seeds on
one dataset, plus baseline training on two more independently generated
datasets with two seeds each. Each has 4,096 training cases, 448 validation
cases and 12 epochs. The test has 3,584 fresh worlds in 56 conditions.
The original 16,384-case study remains separate. Selected and final
checkpoints are both reported. Selection uses withheld Rician likelihood,
not test NMSE. Ordinary variants share example orders and pilot masks.

Relative to ten stages/width 16, selected-checkpoint macro gains are:
  five stages: -1.977 dB; 4.25 ms versus 8.04 ms for ten stages;
  twenty stages: +0.181 dB; 15.60 ms, with MORE severe errors;
  width eight: -0.731 dB; little inference-time saving;
  width 32: +0.065 dB; fewer severe errors, but some cells worsen.

The study answers the sensitivity question, not the optimal-depth or
convergence question. Matrix operations dominate the tiny controller's
cost. No variant improves every condition, and twelve epochs are not
proof of convergence. Conditional intervals exclude training uncertainty
and are not adjusted for the six exploratory comparisons.

3. Estimate noise variance or reference amplitude from observations
Location: main discussion; supplement, "Static Noise and Reference
Estimation," with update equations and a complete seven-condition table.

We implemented a static observation-only EM diagnostic for a shared
positive reference gain and shared complex noise variance. Reference
shape and phase remain supplied. Nine candidate fits use 21 pilots and
are scored on nine withheld pilots; the chosen fit is repeated using all
30. The likelihood includes log(v), which cannot be omitted when choosing
variance. All selection/refit computation is counted; preprocessing alone
averages 45.05 ms before learned estimation. No extra pilots are used.

192 independent worlds are reused across seven input-error conditions
(1,344 condition-cases). Average learned-error reductions are 0.832/0.997
dB for -6/+6 dB variance error, 1.863/1.449 dB for -10/+10% amplitude
error, and 2.872 dB for joint +10%/+6 dB error. However, the worst +6 dB
variance cell worsens by 1.048 dB. Correct inputs can also be worsened.
Unknown reference phase is not recovered: channel and reference can
rotate together without changing magnitudes. This is a limited diagnostic,
not a general calibration fix, adaptive measurement policy or hardware result.

4. Test pilot budgets near P = 2K
Location: main "Operating Limits"; supplement, "Tight Pilot Counts and
Weak References."

We tested P = 6, 8, 10, 12, 20, 30 with K = 3, at SNR -5, 5, 15 dB,
32 cases per cell (576 worlds). Learned models remain frozen: this is
transfer, not pilot-specific retraining. At SNR 5 dB, learned NMSE changes
from -14.332 dB at P=30 to -2.197 dB at P=6. At -5 dB SNR and P=6 it
is +4.089 dB. Full local row-Jacobian rank in these sampled nonzero-reference
cases does not ensure stable finite-noise estimation. At SNR 5 dB, the
median smallest singular value drops from 2.488 to 0.172. We distinguish
the unrestricted-row dimension condition from global sparse-channel
identifiability; neither a general uniqueness theorem nor recovery at
the dimension boundary is claimed.

5. Diagnose weak references and try a curriculum
Location: main discussion; supplement, "Tight Pilot Counts and Weak
References," "A Nearly Noiseless Failure," and the new training study.

Paired reference sweeps distinguish absent-reference phase ambiguity from
nonzero-reference conditioning/optimization difficulties. At SNR 20 dB,
RSR -10 dB, EM gives -1.773 dB from spectral initialization but -21.036 dB
when started at the true channel. True-start EM is an oracle diagnostic,
never a deployable comparator. This gap does not prove global identification
or establish a single cause of all weak-reference failures.

The matched curriculum presents stronger references before weaker ones,
with the same total per-example exposure. It gains 0.206 dB overall and
0.255 dB on RSR 0 dB cells, but loses 2.353 dB in the worst cell (SNR 20,
RSR 5, two paths). Its repeated exposures share an epoch's mask, so it
changes distinct-mask coverage as well as ordering. It is not a pure
ordering intervention or a general remedy. No specialized low-RSR branch
was added; the requested curriculum alternative was the bounded test.

An additional, clearly out-of-range SNR 60 dB check found filtering damage
even at RSR 12 dB: learned NMSE -6.647 dB versus EM100 -65.715 dB.
Disabling filtering but retaining learned steps gives -44.020 dB; starting
the full model at truth still gives -6.653 dB. These post-hoc interventions
support filtering bias in this setting, not a universally safe switching
rule. Independent numerical replay confirms the result. The paper makes
no low-noise consistency claim.

The pilot and weak-reference diagnostics have a documented seed/channel
overlap and are not pooled as independent replications. The new training
test uses a disjoint seed range amended before any test generation.

6. Test a mixture of experts for few paths
Location: main discussion and new training-study supplement section.

A 374-parameter two-expert controller was trained with the same example,
epoch and selection budgets as the 188-parameter baseline and 364-parameter
width-32 control. It improves overall NMSE by only 0.023 dB and two-path
NMSE by 0.179 dB. Width 32 improves those measures by 0.065 and 0.286 dB.
The expert model does not demonstrate an advantage over that similarly
sized plain controller in this experiment. Worst-cell losses are retained.
It mixes controller outputs, not identified physical components; its
initial thresholds also differ, so effects cannot be attributed to gating
alone. The primary estimator is unchanged.

7. Release fuller reproducibility materials
Location: supplement, "Saved-Result Audit" and "Initialization and
Numerical Record"; scientific archive instructions and manifest.

The expanded scientific archive includes the relevant generating code,
saved observations/truth/predictions, selected/final weights, histories,
frozen settings, environment records, figure scripts and SHA-256 manifests.
A separate smaller Overleaf archive contains the paper, supplement,
bibliography and figures. Replay and clean-regeneration commands are
provided; clean regeneration refuses to overwrite existing evidence.

This revision audited all 300 epochs and 2,912 new prediction files,
recomputed headline values and conditional intervals, checked matched
orders/masks and all 50 new checkpoints, regenerated a case in each of
56 test cells, and replayed selected and final models. The largest new
replay difference was below 1.2e-15. Seventeen focused tests pass.
Independent-data baseline averages (-12.450, -12.124, -12.229 dB, two
initializations each) and individual seed scores are reported separately.
These few datasets do not remove training-population uncertainty.

Additional presentation/theory changes
The supplement derives the Rician EM update from the latent complex field
and its conditional von Mises phase distribution. The main text explains
the cubic filter's spectral multiplier and its weak-path bias. Bounds apply
to one correction, not global recovery or convergence of the full network.
The relaxed EM argument is separated from the subsequent learned filter.
Related work distinguishes LRHD/OptShrink, affine phase retrieval, Rician
self-supervision and amplitude-only beam training by their observations
and assumptions. Small parameter count is not presented as low operation
count; matrix operations and implementation-specific timing are disclosed.

Remaining limits before a venue submission
This is a general IEEE-format manuscript, not an accepted or venue-approved
paper. Hardware/atomic-response validation, unknown arbitrary per-antenna
drift, full LRHD or published URformer reproduction, a strong angle-domain
sparse comparator and general convergence remain outside this revision.
CPU/GPU parity has not been tested. The evidence does not prove universal
novelty, practical deployment readiness, or superiority to all structural
methods. Verify author details, the companion manuscript's availability,
page limits and the selected venue's AI-disclosure policy before submission.
