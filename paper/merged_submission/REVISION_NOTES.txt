REVISION RECORD: FINAL OPTSHRINK COMPARISONS

What changed

Final comparison outcome: online in-loop OptShrink rank selection wins
the declared average-cell-dB metric by 0.507 dB (adjusted 95% interval
0.437 to 0.562 dB). The original compact estimator takes 4.96 ms versus
108.70 ms and has fewer errors above NMSE one (3.08% versus 6.87%).
It also has lower pooled linear-error NMSE, but that does not replace
the declared primary metric. The paper now states an accuracy/cost/
error-distribution trade-off, not superiority over the strongest OptShrink.
The added experiment and audit completed in 22.18 minutes.

1. OptShrink is now a primary comparator, not only a separate pilot.
   The paper includes broad offline tuning on the exact original validation
   observations/masks and a further fresh observation-based rank comparison.
   Each study has 4,096 new paired worlds and keeps the original three
   188-parameter checkpoints unchanged. There was no new neural training.

2. The online control searches ranks 1-15 plus no filtering, with in-loop,
   final-only and joint placement/rank selection. It uses 21 pilots to fit,
   nine to score Rician likelihood, then refits on all 30. No channel truth
   or path-count label enters the selector. It inherits mixing strength
   and iteration count from offline validation; it is not an exhaustive
   online search over every possible classical setting.

3. The new comparisons use one PyTorch CPU backend, double precision,
   one thread and batch size one. Reported inference time includes all
   initialization, search, filtering and refitting. Loading and file access
   are excluded. Older mixed-backend timing results are labelled separately.

4. Figures and tables now report per-condition gains, declared adjusted
   average-gain intervals, fixed-budget cost curves, full error distributions,
   three error thresholds, training-seed variation, individual test-bank
   results and excluded stress cases. Full individual-cell values remain
   in the included evidence records. Metric averages are not ensembles.

5. The earlier narrow-population OptShrink win remains in the main paper.
   The initial hybrid failure, corrected hybrid failure, simple stopping/
   filter-release failure, small-budget Transformer limitation and failed
   local error-model screen are retained in the supplement. We did not
   discard them or turn them retrospectively into positive results.

6. The English is simpler, the abstract is qualitative, and the introduction
   has a background paragraph, a motivation paragraph and contributions.
   Methods and assumptions are explained before results. The main paper
   has no forward section roadmap. Technical definitions needed to reproduce
   the estimator remain; simplicity is not achieved by removing assumptions.

7. Publication details were updated for deep Hankel reconstruction and
   Rician-likelihood learning. Existing OptShrink, low-rank Hankel,
   self-supervised and Rydberg work is acknowledged. A recent robust
   Quantum-MUSIC preprint is distinguished from our no-outlier model.
   The paper does not claim a first use of these ingredients.

8. A brief AI-assistance disclosure records substantive drafting, coding
   and figure-script assistance. The author must check its wording and
   the target venue's current policy. This is not a claim that final human
   verification has already taken place.

What the revision does NOT solve

- No convergence or fixed-point/stability theorem for the full alternating
  estimator. The norm and relaxed-EM statements concern individual steps.
- No proof that the cubic shrinker is statistically optimal under the
  dependent Hankel error distribution.
- No hardware, atomic-dynamics, optical-readout or 2D-array validation.
- Known noise and reference remain main-model assumptions. Existing
  mismatch simulations do not establish robustness to unknown drift.
- Filtering bias remains severe in the nearly noiseless stress test.
- The 21-input/30-input training/deployment difference is stated but not
  causally isolated by a matched retraining experiment.
- The small Transformer pilot is not a strong trained baseline or a
  reproduction of published URformer. Full LRHD/HVaF/LRPR-VBL comparisons
  are not claimed.
- Two fresh test banks do not measure independent retraining uncertainty.
- Passing an accuracy screen does not prove novelty or guarantee acceptance.
