\begin{abstract}
Rydberg atomic quantum receivers measure only the \emph{magnitude} of the
incident field superposed on a local reference, making multi-user channel
estimation a biased phase-retrieval rather than a linear-Gaussian problem. The
known solvers---biased Gerchberg--Saxton (GS) and its expectation--maximisation refinement EM-GS---treat the channel as unstructured, and their
normalised mean-square error (NMSE) consequently does not improve as the
atomic array grows. We observe that on a uniform linear array each propagation
path contributes exactly one spatial complex exponential, so the Hankel
lifting of every channel column has rank at most the path count. Interleaving
a Cadzow low-rank Hankel projection with every exact EM-GS update yields HS-GS; the model order comes from a held-out pilot residual, no oracle path count
is used, and the magnitude observation is never linearised. We also derive
unconstrained and geometry-constrained Cram\'er--Rao bounds for the exact
Rician likelihood. Over $3.6\times10^{4}$ paired trials, and \emph{under the tested sparse
geometric multipath model with i.i.d.\ uniform angles of arrival and equal
per-path power}, HS-GS improves on EM-GS by a mean of $2.85$~dB at $N=32$.
Plotting the two error curves separately shows why: EM-GS is flat in array
size to within $0.04$~dB, so HS-GS is the only estimator whose NMSE improves
as the aperture grows, and the gap widens monotonically
($0.03\to0.81\to2.45$~dB at $N=8/16/32$). The benefit is conditional rather
than general: at $N=8$ the projection helps by $0.74$~dB at $-10$~dB SNR but
\emph{hurts} by up to $0.40$~dB above $0$~dB. A controlled path-count
experiment confirms the mechanism: the gain decays monotonically from
$7.04$~dB at $L=2$ to zero exactly as the path count reaches the Hankel rank
ceiling. Robustness under clustered
propagation is not established and is the decisive next test.
\end{abstract}

%==========================================================================
\section{Introduction}
%==========================================================================
Rydberg atomic quantum receivers replace the RF front end with optically read
excited atoms, giving sensitivity and bandwidth flexibility unavailable to
conventional antennas \cite{cui2025,fancher2021}. Cui \emph{et
al.} \cite{cui2025} showed the resulting atomic MIMO receiver does not obey
the linear transmission model: heterodyne readout against a local reference
yields the \emph{modulus} of the summed field, making recovery a \emph{biased
phase-retrieval} problem \cite{netrapalli2015,dong2023}. They adapted
Gerchberg--Saxton (GS) \cite{gerchberg1972} to this setting and proposed
EM-GS, replacing the hard modulus substitution with the exact posterior mean
of the phase factor.

Both solvers treat $\bG\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times K}$ as $2NK$ free real
parameters. Under the magnitude model the Fisher information is block diagonal
across receive elements (Section~\ref{sec:crlb}), so estimation separates into
$N$ per-row problems: enlarging the array adds as many unknowns as
measurements and leaves the \emph{normalised} error unchanged---EM-GS varies
by only $0.012$~dB across $N\in\{8,16,32\}$. Any improvement scaling with
array size must come from structure coupling the rows.

Such structure exists whenever the array is a ULA and propagation is via a few
resolvable paths: each path imprints one spatial complex exponential across
the aperture. Grid-based and subspace estimators exploit this in conventional
MIMO, but it has not been used inside a magnitude-only atomic receiver, where
the non-linearity forbids linear least squares.

\textbf{Contributions.} We show the Hankel lifting of each ULA channel column
has rank at most $L_k$, turning path sparsity into a grid-free low-rank
constraint needing no angle estimation (\S\ref{sec:hankel}); propose HS-GS,
interleaving a Cadzow projection with every exact EM-GS update at an order
chosen from a held-out pilot residual, not oracle knowledge
(\S\ref{sec:method}); derive the unconstrained and geometry-constrained CRLBs
for this Rician likelihood, correcting a rank-2 misconstruction of the
per-measurement Fisher information (\S\ref{sec:crlb}); and isolate \emph{when}
the method works with a controlled path-count experiment
(\S\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:limits}).

\textbf{Notation.} $(\cdot)^{T}$ and $(\cdot)^{H}$ are transpose and conjugate
transpose; $|\cdot|$ on a matrix is elementwise modulus; $\odot$ is the Hadamard
product; $\|\cdot\|_F$ is the Frobenius norm; $I_\nu$ is the order-$\nu$
modified Bessel function of the first kind.

\section{System Model}\label{sec:model}
%==========================================================================
\subsection{Magnitude-Only Measurement}
$K$ single-antenna users transmit to an $N$-element atomic ULA over $P$
pilots. With $\bG\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times K}$ the channel,
$\bS\in\mathbb{C}^{K\times P}$ the pilots, $\bB\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times P}$ the
reference and $\bW\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times P}$ the noise, the receiver observes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:obs}
\bZ=\bigl|\bG\bS+\bB+\bW\bigr|\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^{N\times P},
\end{equation}
with $\bW$ i.i.d.\ $\CN(0,\sigma^2)$ added \emph{inside} the modulus. The
phase of $\bG\bS+\bB$ is unobservable; only $\bB$ makes $\bG$ identifiable,
through the cross term of $|x+b|^2=|x|^2+2\Re\{xb^*\}+|b|^2$. Following
\cite{cui2025}, $\bB$ is constant over $(n,p)$ with zero phase, the SNR fixes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:snr}
\sigma^2=K/\mathrm{SNR}_{\mathrm{lin}},
\end{equation}
and the reference-to-signal ratio (RSR) is defined against a \emph{single}
user, giving $|b|=\sqrt{\mathrm{RSR}_{\mathrm{lin}}}$. Since $K$ users
transmit at once, a nominal $12$~dB RSR is $7.15$~dB against the $K$-user
total; all RSR values here are the single-user ones.

\subsection{Geometric ULA Channel}
The channel is generated as sparse geometric multipath. With half-wavelength
spacing the electrical angle of path $\ell$ of user $k$ is
$\psi_{\ell,k}=\pi\sin\theta_{\ell,k}$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chan}
g_{n,k}=c\sum_{\ell=1}^{L_k}\alpha_{\ell,k}\,
e^{-\jmath(n-1)\psi_{\ell,k}},\qquad n=1,\dots,N,
\end{equation}
with atomic gain $c=1$ (so the electrical and atomic channels coincide),
$\alpha_{\ell,k}\sim\CN(0,\beta_k/L_k)$ and $\beta_k=1$.

The assumptions used throughout: sparse geometric multipath with
$L_k\sim\mathcal{U}\{3,\dots,7\}$ i.i.d.\ per user per realisation; angles of
arrival i.i.d.\ \emph{uniform in $\theta$} (Kolmogorov--Smirnov over $4465$
samples: $p=0.150$ against uniform-in-$\theta$, $p<10^{-4}$ against
uniform-in-$\sin\theta$); equal per-path power; pilots i.i.d.\ $\CN(0,1)$,
redrawn each trial and rejection-sampled to full row rank with $P\ge2K$, hence
\emph{not} orthogonal (largest off-diagonal $|\bS\bS^{H}|/P$ is $0.233$); and
flat, narrowband propagation. The channel is \emph{not} a clustered 3GPP TR 38.901
or Saleh--Valenzuela model; Section~\ref{sec:limits} addresses the
consequences. Table~\ref{tab:params} collects the parameters.

\subsection{Baseline Solvers}
Row $n$ of \eqref{eq:obs} depends only on row $n$ of $\bG$, so it reduces to
$N$ independent biased PR problems of dimension $K$. Biased GS \cite{cui2025}
alternates a least-squares update with the hard modulus substitution
$\hat{\mathbf{y}}=\bZ\odot e^{\jmath\angle(\bG\bS+\bB)}$; EM-GS instead uses
the exact conditional mean of the phase factor, introducing the Bessel ratio
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bessel}
R(\kappa)=\frac{I_1(\kappa)}{I_0(\kappa)},\qquad
\kappa=\frac{2}{\sigma^2}\,\bZ\odot\bigl|\bG\bS+\bB\bigr| ,
\end{equation}
so the restored observation is shrunk by $R(\kappa)\in[0,1)$ according to
measurement reliability. As $\kappa\to\infty$, $R\to1$ and EM-GS provably
reduces to biased GS---a limit reached in practice at high RSR
(\S\ref{sec:results}-A).

%==========================================================================
\section{Hankel Structure of the ULA Channel}\label{sec:hankel}
%==========================================================================
Drop the user index and write one channel column as
$g_n=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\alpha_\ell z_\ell^{\,n}$ with
$z_\ell=e^{-\jmath\psi_\ell}$. Define the Hankel lifting with pencil parameter
$p$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lift}
\Hank(\bg)=
\begin{bmatrix}
g_0 & g_1 & \cdots & g_{p}\\g_1 & g_2 & \cdots & g_{p+1}\\
\vdots & & \ddots & \vdots\\g_{N-p-1} & \cdots & & g_{N-1}
\end{bmatrix}
\in\mathbb{C}^{(N-p)\times(p+1)} .
\end{equation}
Its $(q,r)$ entry is $g_{q+r}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sep}
\bigl[\Hank(\bg)\bigr]_{q,r}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\alpha_\ell z_\ell^{\,q+r}
=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\alpha_\ell\,z_\ell^{\,q}\,z_\ell^{\,r},
\end{equation}
separating in $q$ and $r$. With $\bv_\ell=[1,z_\ell,\dots,z_\ell^{N-p-1}]^{T}$
and $\bw_\ell=[1,z_\ell,\dots,z_\ell^{p}]^{T}$ this is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank1}
\Hank(\bg)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\alpha_\ell\,\bv_\ell\bw_\ell^{T},
\end{equation}
a sum of $L$ rank-one terms, hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank}
\boxed{\ \rank\bigl(\Hank(\bg)\bigr)\le L.\ }
\end{equation}
\emph{Each propagation path creates one spatial complex exponential across the
ULA and therefore contributes exactly one rank-one component to the Hankel
lifting}, converting path sparsity into low-rank matrix structure without
discretising the angular domain. When the $z_\ell$ are distinct and $L$ does
not exceed the matrix dimensions, \eqref{eq:rank} holds with equality, and
conversely a sequence whose Hankel matrix has rank $L$ is a sum of $L$
exponentials \cite{cadzow1988}.

The constraint has a ceiling: $\Hank(\bg)$ being $(N-p)\times(p+1)$, its rank
cannot exceed $\min(N-p,p+1)$, maximised over $p$ at
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cap}
r_{\max}=\max_{1\le p\le N-1}\min(N-p,\,p+1)=\lceil N/2\rceil ,
\end{equation}
so $r_{\max}=4,8,16$ for $N=8,16,32$. If $L\ge r_{\max}$ then $\rank\le L$
holds for every vector and carries no information---the ceiling states
precisely \emph{when} the prior is informative, and \S\ref{sec:results}-C
tests it directly.

\section{Proposed HS-GS Estimator}\label{sec:method}
%==========================================================================
\subsection{Cadzow Projection}
Let $\mathcal{S}_{\hat L}$ be the length-$N$ sequences whose Hankel lifting
has rank at most $\hat L$. Membership is the intersection of two sets with
exact projectors: fixed-rank matrices (truncated SVD, Frobenius-optimal by
Eckart--Young) and Hankel matrices (anti-diagonal averaging). Cadzow's algorithm \cite{cadzow1988} alternates them: build
$\bH\leftarrow\Hank(\bg)$; take its SVD
$\bH=\bU\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^{H}$; retain the $\hat L$ dominant
components as $\bH_{\mathrm{lr}}=\bU_{:,1:\hat L}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{1:\hat L}\mathbf{V}_{:,1:\hat L}^{H}$; restore Hankel structure by averaging each
anti-diagonal, $\bg\leftarrow\Hank^{\dagger}(\bH_{\mathrm{lr}})$; and repeat
for $n_{\mathrm{cz}}=4$ sweeps.
The intersection is a non-convex variety and four sweeps do not reach its exact
nearest point, so $\mathcal{P}_{\hat L}$ is an \emph{approximate} projection
(Section~\ref{sec:results}-D quantifies the residual) and the iteration below
carries no convergence guarantee. When $\hat L\ge r_{\max}$ the routine returns
its input unchanged, so HS-GS reproduces EM-GS bit for bit.

\subsection{Interleaved Iteration and Order Selection}
The projection acts \emph{inside} the exact iteration, not as post-processing:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:map}
\bG^{(t)}=\mathcal{P}_{\hat L}\circ\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{EM\text{-}GS}}
\bigl(\bG^{(t-1)}\bigr),\qquad t=1,\dots,T,
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{P}_{\hat L}$ acting column-wise after \emph{every} one of the
$T=50$ updates. This matters because EM-GS update quality depends on the
current iterate through $\kappa$ in \eqref{eq:bessel}: a structurally cleaner
iterate yields a better phase estimate, hence a better next iterate.
Algorithms~\ref{alg:emgs}--\ref{alg:hsgs} give the measurement update, the
projection, the rank selector and their composition; the paragraph after
Alg.~\ref{alg:hsgs} states precisely which lines separate HS-GS from the
baseline.

HS-GS needs a target rank $\hat L$, and the true $L_k$ is unknown to any
practical receiver. The in-sample residual cannot supply it: constraining
$\bG$ to a smaller set can only worsen the fit to the pilots used for fitting,
so that criterion always prefers the largest candidate. Splitting the pilots
into a fitting set and a validation set of fraction $\nu=0.3$ penalises
over-modelling correctly, and every quantity involved is observable:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:order}
\hat L=\arg\min_{1\le L\le r_{\max}}
\bigl\|\,\bZ_{\mathcal{V}}-\bigl|\bG_L\bS_{\mathcal{V}}+\bB_{\mathcal{V}}\bigr|\,\bigr\|_F^{2},
\end{equation}
with $\bG_L$ the estimate at order $L$ from the fitting columns only, run for
$T_{\mathrm{sel}}=20$ iterations. \textbf{No oracle rank is used anywhere in
this paper}; the selector returns a single scalar $\hat L$ applied to all $K$
columns. Order matters both ways: $\hat L<L$ truncates genuine components, a
bias that does not vanish as noise decreases, while $\hat L>L$ admits noise
and weakens the prior. Our selector systematically under-selects---true orders
$(7,4,6)$ typically give $\hat L=5$, and in Section~\ref{sec:results}-C mean
$\hat L$ reaches only $11.63$ at $L=16$.

\begin{algorithm}[!tb]
\caption{EM-GS measurement update $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{EM}}$ (one iteration)}
\label{alg:emgs}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\bZ,\bS,\bB,\sigma^2$; iterate $\bG$ ($\varnothing$ $\Rightarrow$ spectral init)
\State $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\leftarrow\bG\bS+\bB$;\quad
       $\boldsymbol{\kappa}\leftarrow(2/\sigma^2)\,\bZ\odot|\boldsymbol{\Lambda}|$
\State $\bR\leftarrow R(\boldsymbol{\kappa})$, $R(x)=I_1(x)/I_0(x)$
       \Comment{elementwise}
\State $\hat\bY\leftarrow\bR\odot\bZ\odot e^{\jmath\angle\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}$
       \Comment{Rician posterior mean}
\State $\bG_{n,:}\leftarrow(\hat\bY_{n,:}-\bB_{n,:})\bS^{H}(\bS\bS^{H})^{-1}$,
       $\ n=1..N$ \Comment{rows independent}
\State \Return $\bG$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[!tb]
\caption{Cadzow projection $\mathcal{P}_{\hat L}$ of one channel column}
\label{alg:cadzow}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\bg\in\mathbb{C}^{N}$, rank $\hat L$, sweeps $n_{\mathrm{cz}}$
\State $p\leftarrow\arg\max_{p}\min(N-p,p+1)$;\quad
       $r_{\max}\leftarrow\lceil N/2\rceil$
\If{$\hat L\ge r_{\max}$} \Return $\bg$
       \Comment{constraint vacuous: exact no-op} \EndIf
\For{$t=1..n_{\mathrm{cz}}$}
  \State $\bH\leftarrow\Hank(\bg)$, $\bH_{q,r}=g_{q+r}$
         \Comment{$(N{-}p)\times(p{+}1)$}
  \State $\bU\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^{H}\leftarrow\mathrm{svd}(\bH)$;\ \ 
         $\bH\leftarrow\bU_{:,1:\hat L}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{1:\hat L}
         \mathbf{V}_{:,1:\hat L}^{H}$ \Comment{rank}
  \State $g_m\leftarrow|\mathcal{A}_m|^{-1}\!\!\sum_{(q,r)\in\mathcal{A}_m}\!\!\bH_{q,r}$,
         $\mathcal{A}_m\!=\!\{q{+}r{=}m\}$ \Comment{Hankel}
\EndFor
\State \Return $\bg$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[!tb]
\caption{HS-GS, and the held-out rank selection it uses}
\label{alg:hsgs}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{HS-GS}{$\bZ,\bS,\bB,\sigma^2$; $\hat L$, $T$}
  \State $\mathrm{active}\leftarrow(\hat L<r_{\max})$;\quad $\bG\leftarrow\varnothing$
  \For{$t=1..T$}
    \State $\bG\leftarrow\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{EM}}(\bG)$
           \Comment{Alg.~\ref{alg:emgs}: \emph{identical} to the baseline}
    \If{$\mathrm{active}$ \textbf{and} $t\bmod\Pi=0$}
      \State $\bG_{:,k}\leftarrow\mathcal{P}_{\hat L}(\bG_{:,k})$, $k=1..K$
             \Comment{Alg.~\ref{alg:cadzow}}
    \EndIf
  \EndFor
  \State \Return $\bG$
\EndFunction
\Statex
\Function{SelectRank}{$\bZ,\bS,\bB,\sigma^2$; $\nu$, $T_{\mathrm{sel}}$}
  \State $\mathcal{V}\leftarrow$ last $\lceil\nu P\rceil$ pilot columns;\quad
         $\mathcal{F}\leftarrow$ the rest
  \For{$L=1..r_{\max}$}
    \State $\bG_L\leftarrow\textsc{HS-GS}(\bZ_{:,\mathcal{F}},\bS_{:,\mathcal{F}},
           \bB_{:,\mathcal{F}},\sigma^2;L,T_{\mathrm{sel}})$
    \State $J(L)\leftarrow\|\bZ_{:,\mathcal{V}}-|\bG_L\bS_{:,\mathcal{V}}
           +\bB_{:,\mathcal{V}}|\|_F^{2}$
           \Comment{columns never fitted}
  \EndFor
  \State \Return $\hat L=\arg\min_L J(L)$
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\noindent\textbf{Relation to the baseline.} Deleting lines 5--7 of
Alg.~\ref{alg:hsgs} yields baseline EM-GS exactly: both chain the same
$\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{EM}}$ for the same $T$ iterations from the same
initialisation, with no early stopping in either. Setting $\hat L\ge r_{\max}$
makes $\mathcal{P}_{\hat L}$ the identity (Alg.~\ref{alg:cadzow}, line 2), so
the two then agree \emph{bit for bit}; this is checked numerically in
\S\ref{sec:setup}.

\subsection{Complexity}
One EM-GS update costs $O(NPK)$; the projection adds $Kn_{\mathrm{cz}}$ SVDs
per iteration, $O(Kn_{\mathrm{cz}}N^{3})$, and selection multiplies this by
the $r_{\max}$ candidates. Against a \emph{chained} EM-GS baseline (the
correct reference, HS-GS issuing $T$ single-iteration calls costing
$1.005$--$1.038\times$ in overhead alone) HS-GS at fixed order costs $1.49$,
$1.32$, $1.22\times$ at $N=8,16,32$---the ratio \emph{falls} with $N$.
Selection dominates the full estimator ($57.7$--$85.5\%$ of runtime), so
shrinking the candidate set is the obvious practical target.

\section{Performance Benchmark}\label{sec:crlb}
%==========================================================================
\subsection{Likelihood and Fisher Information}
Conditional on $\bG,\bS,\bB$ the noiseless field at $(n,p)$ is a known
constant $\lambda=(\bG\bS+\bB)_{n,p}$, so $z=|\lambda+w|$ is Rice distributed:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rice}
p(z\mid\lambda)=\frac{2z}{\sigma^2}
\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{z^2+|\lambda|^2}{\sigma^2}\Bigr)
I_0\!\Bigl(\frac{2z|\lambda|}{\sigma^2}\Bigr).
\end{equation}
Crucially \eqref{eq:rice} depends on $\lambda$ \emph{only through
$a=|\lambda|$}. Differentiating, with $\frac{d}{dx}\log I_0(x)=R(x)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score}
\frac{\partial\log p}{\partial a}=\frac{2}{\sigma^2}\bigl[zR(\kappa)-a\bigr],
\qquad \kappa=\frac{2za}{\sigma^2},
\end{equation}
so one measurement carries scalar information
$\beta(a,\sigma^2)=\mathbb{E}[(\partial_a\log p)^2]$, contributing to the
Fisher matrix in real coordinates
$\boldsymbol{\eta}=[\Re\mathrm{vec}\,\bG;\Im\mathrm{vec}\,\bG]$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fisher}
\bJ_{n}=\sum_{p=1}^{P}4\beta(a_{n,p},\sigma^2)\,
\mathbf{u}_{n,p}\mathbf{u}_{n,p}^{T},
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{u}_{n,p}=\partial a_{n,p}/\partial\boldsymbol{\eta}_n$.
\textbf{Each magnitude measurement therefore contributes rank-one information,
not rank two.} Crediting both quadratures overstates it and yields a bound too
low by $0.17$--$4.64$~dB across our $36$ points. An identifiability check
settles the form: with $K=3$ and $P=3$ the per-row problem is unidentifiable
and \eqref{eq:fisher} is correctly singular, whereas the rank-two construction
claims full rank; Fig.~\ref{fig:snr} corroborates it, EM-GS sitting within
$0.05$~dB of the rank-one bound at high SNR. Because row $n$ of $\bG\bS$
enters only measurement row $n$, $\bJ$ is block diagonal across receive
elements---which is why the unconstrained bound, and any estimator attaining
it, is flat in $N$ under a normalised metric.

\subsection{Constrained Bound on the Geometric Manifold}
The unconstrained bound governs estimators treating $\bG$ as $2NK$ free
parameters. HS-GS instead exploits \eqref{eq:chan}, whose parameters $\boldsymbol{\phi}=\{\psi_{\ell,k},\Re\alpha_{\ell,k},\Im\alpha_{\ell,k}\}\in\mathbb{R}
^{3\sum_k L_k}$ number about $45$, \emph{independent of $N$}. With
$\bD=\partial\boldsymbol{\eta}/\partial\boldsymbol{\phi}$ the textbook form
$\bD(\bD^{T}\bJ\bD)^{-1}\bD^{T}$ is unusable, $\bD$ being rank deficient: at
$N=8$ its mean numerical rank is $40.4$ against $\approx44.7$ parameters, and
$28.1\%$ of realisations have $3\sum_k L_k>2NK$, so
$\boldsymbol{\phi}\mapsto\bG$ is not injective. We use the Gorman--Hero /
Stoica--Ng tangent-space form \cite{gorman1990,stoica1998}, $\bU$ an
orthonormal basis of $\range(\bD)$ from SVD at tolerance $10^{-9}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ccrb}
\mathrm{CCRB}=\bU\bigl(\bU^{T}\bJ\bU\bigr)^{-1}\bU^{T},
\end{equation}
which depends on the tangent \emph{space} and is invariant to
over-parameterisation. Worst conditioning of $\bU^{T}\bJ\bU$ is then $80.1$
(at the degenerate $P=6$), elsewhere below $35$. Both bounds are averaged over
the same $400$ realisations as the estimator curves.

\subsection{Bias Caveat}
Both bounds constrain the covariance of \emph{unbiased} estimators, whereas
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mse}
\mathrm{MSE}=\mathrm{variance}+\mathrm{bias}^2 .
\end{equation}
All three estimators run to a fixed budget with the shrinking update
\eqref{eq:bessel}, and HS-GS additionally truncates rank; none is unbiased, so
neither bound applies strictly. The unconstrained CRLB is therefore the
reference for GS and EM-GS only---HS-GS uses prior information it does not
model---and the CCRB, itself an unbiased-estimation bound, does not forbid a
sufficiently biased structured estimator from falling below it.
\S\ref{sec:results}-E reports both effects.

%==========================================================================
\section{Simulation Setup}\label{sec:setup}
%==========================================================================
All results use the exact observation \eqref{eq:obs}; the modulus is never
linearised, which is verified per trial. NMSE is pooled as a ratio of sums,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nmse}
\mathrm{NMSE}_G=10\log_{10}
\frac{\sum_{t}\|\hat\bG_t-\bG_t\|_F^2}{\sum_{t}\|\bG_t\|_F^2},
\end{equation}
never a mean of per-trial decibels: \eqref{eq:nmse} estimates the ensemble
quantity a CRLB bounds, whereas a mean-of-decibels is smaller by Jensen. The
factor is $10$, not $20$, the arguments being energies. Within a point all
estimators see bit-identical realisations, so the gain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gain}
\Delta_{\mathrm{HS}}=10\log_{10}
\frac{\sum_t\|\hat\bG^{\mathrm{EM}}_t-\bG_t\|_F^2}
     {\sum_t\|\hat\bG^{\mathrm{HS}}_t-\bG_t\|_F^2}
\end{equation}
and its $95\%$ paired bootstrap interval ($2000$ resamples) benefit from
cancellation of the common channel draw; realisations are independent
\emph{across} points.

\textbf{Two averaging domains, kept distinct.} Within an operating point
\eqref{eq:nmse} sums energies first and converts last, averaging in the
\emph{linear} domain; across SNR (Fig.~\ref{fig:panels}, bottom right) the
mean is of the per-SNR decibel values, \emph{after} conversion. Linear NMSE
spans three orders of magnitude here, so a linear average across SNR is
dominated by the lowest-SNR point ($\approx70\%$ of the sum against $0.72\%$
for the top three) and nearly blind to the high-SNR half---precisely where the
projection hurts at $N=8$.

\textbf{Trial budget.} Rank selection reruns the estimator once per candidate
order, so a trial at $N=32$ costs $\approx8.5\times$ one at $N=8$; the budget
is tiered and every point reports its own count. \S\ref{sec:results} rests on
$2.5\times10^{4}$ trials for the SNR and pilot-length sweeps, $8.4\times10^{3}$
on the $N\times$SNR grid of Fig.~\ref{fig:panels}, and $2.4\times10^{3}$ on the
path-count sweep.

\textbf{Verification.} A standalone package reproduces the comparison and
passes thirteen automated checks. Two are load-bearing for fairness and
executed rather than asserted: each trial hands both estimators one
\emph{read-only} world $(\bG,\bS,\bB,\bW,\bZ)$, so they cannot diverge, and
with the projection disabled HS-GS reproduces EM-GS to
$\max|\mathrm{diff}|=0$---the projection is provably the only difference. A
third confirms \S\ref{sec:hankel}: a noiseless $L$-path column has
$\sigma_{L+1}/\sigma_1\approx7\times10^{-16}$.

\subsection{Main Result and Array-Size Scaling} First the operating point. EM-GS improves on biased GS by $1.31$~dB at RSR
$=0$~dB, collapsing to $0.113$~dB at $12$~dB and $0.002$~dB at $24$~dB as
$R(\kappa)\to1$ and the two provably coincide. RSR $=12$~dB, from
\cite{cui2025}, keeps the baselines separable, so the gains below are
attributable to the projection, not the choice of exact step.
Fig.~\ref{fig:snr} shows the comparison at $N=32$, $P=30$. EM-GS tracks the unconstrained CRLB to within $0.05$~dB for SNR $\ge5$~dB, so
it is close to efficient for the unstructured problem and the corrected bound
is credible. The geometric CCRB lies $0.87$--$9.98$~dB below it, quantifying
what the structure is worth in principle; HS-GS occupies part of that gap,
improving on EM-GS by $2.27$--$3.04$~dB across the sweep.

Fig.~\ref{fig:scaling} isolates the scaling. Averaged over the twelve
operating points at each array size the gain is $-0.19$, $+0.78$, $+2.85$~dB
for $N=8,16,32$, win rate $33.5$, $74.0$, $95.3\%$, constraint active in
$57.5$, $92.7$, $99.6\%$ of trials; the largest single point is $4.33$~dB.
Under the same averaging EM-GS varies by only $0.012$~dB, as \S\ref{sec:crlb}
requires: the $N$-dependence belongs to the structural prior, not a degrading
baseline.

The mechanism is the ratio of path count to rank ceiling \eqref{eq:cap}: at
$N=8$, $r_{\max}=4$ while $L_k\in\{3,\dots,7\}$, so the projection is inactive
in $43\%$ of trials and where active often truncates real components, whereas
at $N=32$, $r_{\max}=16\gg L_k$ and it is active in $99.6\%$. At $N=8$ the sign of any scalar summary depends on the
pooling---the per-point mean is $-0.19$~dB, energy-pooling gives
$+0.81$~dB---because HS-GS wins at low SNR and loses at high SNR there. That
high-SNR loss is a bias-dominated floor: truncation bias does not shrink with
noise, so as variance falls the trade stops paying. At $N=16$ and $32$ every
pooling agrees in sign.

\begin{figure}[!tb]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.97\columnwidth]{fig/fig3_pathcount.pdf}
\caption{Controlled path count ($N=32$, $P=30$, SNR $=5$~dB, RSR $=12$~dB,
$300$ trials per point, $L$ fixed and identical across users). (a) EM-GS is

\label{fig:pathcount}

\subsection{Controlled Path Count: When and Why the Method Works}
Every experiment above draws $L_k$ at random, so none isolates path count.
Fig.~\ref{fig:pathcount} fixes $L_k=L$ across users and sweeps it to
$r_{\max}$; the gain decays strictly monotonically,
\[
7.04,\;3.56,\;1.79,\;1.04,\;0.58,\;0.27,\;0.05,\;-0.12~\text{dB}
\]
for $L=2,4,\dots,16$, with the per-trial win rate falling from $100\%$ to
$45.0\%$ and EM-GS flat to $0.19$~dB across the sweep. The interpretation is direct: when $L\ll r_{\max}$ the lifting carries
substantial redundant structure, so projecting suppresses error that does not
share it; as $L\to r_{\max}$ the prior admits almost every vector and the
benefit disappears.

Two details matter. The null is reached at $L=14$ (gain $+0.05$~dB, CI
$[-0.05,+0.14]$), one grid point \emph{before} the ceiling, and at $L=16$ it
is small but significantly negative ($-0.12$~dB, CI $[-0.21,-0.04]$). The
selector explains this, not the theory: mean $\hat L$ reaches only $11.63$ at
$L=16$, so the constraint is still active in $89.3\%$ of trials and there
discards genuine components---the $\hat L<L$ regime of \S\ref{sec:method}-B.
Second, this rules out the obvious rival: generic denoising would persist at
large $L$, where the estimate is equally noisy, not vanish exactly where the
prior becomes vacuous.

\subsection{Structural Diagnostic}
Fig.~\ref{fig:spectrum} shows why the prior exists and what the projection
achieves. The true channel's Hankel spectrum falls to $\sim\!10^{-16}$
immediately after index $L$: the low-rank property is exact, as
\eqref{eq:rank} states. The EM-GS estimate has no such cliff---noise fills the
spectrum, so the estimate is not a sum of $L$ exponentials. After Cadzow a
cliff reappears at the correct index but only to $\sim\!10^{-3}$; that
shortfall is the four-sweep projection being approximate, and it upper-bounds
how much structure can be imposed---consistent with HS-GS attaining only part
of the CCRB headroom.

\subsection{Absolute NMSE of Both Estimators versus $N$}
Fig.~\ref{fig:scaling} plots the \emph{difference} $\Delta_{\mathrm{HS}}$,
leaving open how each estimator behaves alone; Fig.~\ref{fig:panels} plots the
two NMSE curves themselves, at $P=30$ over an SNR grid extended to $-10$~dB.

Two facts separate cleanly. \textbf{EM-GS is flat in $N$}: SNR-averaged
$-10.321$, $-10.337$, $-10.301$~dB at $N=8,16,32$, a spread of $0.036$~dB, and
equally flat inside every panel---a larger array buys the unstructured
estimator essentially nothing, as the block-diagonal Fisher structure
requires. \textbf{HS-GS is the only curve that moves}: $-10.350$, $-11.149$,
$-12.753$~dB, so the gap widens monotonically, $0.03\to0.81\to2.45$~dB, at
every SNR tested.

The $N=8$ panel needs its own sentence: the SNR-averaged $0.03$~dB conceals a
sign change, HS-GS gaining $0.744$~dB at $-10$~dB but losing
$0.190$--$0.403$~dB at $0$~dB and above, intervals excluding zero both ways.
$N=8$ is a mixed result, not a small positive one.

\subsection{Distance to the Bounds, and Crossings}
HS-GS lies above the geometric CCRB at $34$ of $36$ points, by
$0.32$--$8.85$~dB, and the gap \emph{grows} with $N$ ($+0.32$--$1.41$~dB at
$N=8$, $+4.29$--$4.82$~dB at $N=32$, $P=30$): it captures more structural gain
in absolute terms as the array grows but a smaller share of what is available.

Curves do fall below the plotted bounds, in two distinct senses. HS-GS lies
below the \emph{unconstrained} CRLB at $26$ of $36$ points, by up to
$6.94$~dB, but this is no violation: that bound governs estimators treating
$\bG$ as $2NK$ free parameters, whereas HS-GS exploits a prior it does not
model. Its proper bound is the CCRB, respected at $34$ of $36$. The remaining
crossings are consistent with bias, as \eqref{eq:mse} permits, and track the
measured shrinkage $c=\langle\hat\bG,\bG\rangle/\|\bG\|_F^{2}$ in both
magnitude and location: GS and EM-GS cross by up to $2.62$~dB at $-5$~dB where
$c_{\mathrm{EM}}=0.944$, but by at most $0.05$~dB for SNR $\ge15$~dB where
$c\to1$; HS-GS falls below the CCRB at exactly the two points of strongest
shrinkage ($c_{\mathrm{HS}}=0.561$, $0.776$). We do not claim bias accounts
for every crossing.

%==========================================================================
\section{Limitations and Future Work}\label{sec:limits}
%==========================================================================
All results here come from the i.i.d.\ uniform-AoA, equal-per-path-power, flat
geometric model of \S\ref{sec:model}, so the defensible claim is
model-conditional: \emph{under the tested sparse geometric multipath model,
exploiting the low-rank Hankel structure of the ULA response improves
channel-estimation NMSE relative to EM-GS, by a mean of $2.85$~dB at $N=32$.}
Nothing here establishes robustness under clustered propagation, and the
decisive next experiment is to repeat the principal comparison under a
clustered 3GPP TR 38.901 generator \cite{tr38901}---a falsification test, not a
confirmation exercise. Under clustering the $z_\ell$ of a cluster are tightly
packed, so $\Hank(\bg)$ is only \emph{numerically} low rank, with a decaying
spectrum rather than an exact cliff, and hard truncation becomes a
bias-inducing approximation. Since the $N=8$ high-SNR degradation already
traces to truncation bias, clustering could plausibly weaken or eliminate the
gain.

Further, a single scalar $\hat L$ is imposed on all $K$ users despite their
differing orders; \eqref{eq:map} has no convergence guarantee, its constraint
set being non-convex and its projection approximate; and all users have equal
power, so near--far effects are untested.

%==========================================================================
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
%==========================================================================
A sparse geometric ULA channel makes each column's Hankel lifting
rank-deficient, and this can be enforced inside an exact biased
phase-retrieval iteration without linearising the magnitude observation or
estimating any angle. HS-GS improves on EM-GS by a mean of $2.85$~dB at $N=32$
under the tested model; absolute NMSE makes the asymmetry explicit, the
unstructured baselines being flat in array size so HS-GS alone converts a
larger aperture into lower normalised error. That the gap widens with $N$ is an
\emph{empirical} finding over $N\in\{8,16,32\}$, consistent with $\lceil
N/2\rceil$ growing past $L_k$ but not proved beyond the sizes simulated; the
benefit is conditional, negative at $N=8$ above $0$~dB SNR. The path-count
sweep delimits the mechanism: the gain scales with $r_{\max}/L$ and vanishes at
the ceiling. Validation under clustered propagation remains the essential next
step.
