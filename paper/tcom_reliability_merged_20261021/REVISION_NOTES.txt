CONTRIBUTION
The contribution is a particular complete receiver and its evaluated
operating limits: exact Rician measurement updates, a compact
self-supervised controller for smooth spatial Hankel filtering, and
observable recovery checks that allocate a distinct algebraic start and
EM effort in the weak-reference/high-SNR region. An explicit intensity
regression construction, its noise covariance and pilot-rank limitations
explain this recovery route. A matched evaluation measures channel error,
communication errors and complete inference cost on the same observations.

EM, Hankel shrinkage, self-supervised learning, affine phase retrieval and
algebraic lifting all have precedents. The paper does not claim their
invention, first-ever learned Rydberg estimation, universal superiority,
full-estimator convergence, guaranteed BER, or a new hardware receiver.
Published URformer and the closest classical precedents are discussed.

CURRENT RESULTS AND LIMITS
The final 153-condition comparison gives mean-cell NMSE -38.620 dB and BER
0.522496%, with slower-window full-grid p90 7.254 ms (weak-target 7.031 ms).
Mean NMSE improves by 0.507 dB over the frozen prior and 0.762 dB over the
full repaired receiver. Ordinary-SNR predictions are exactly preserved.
The weak-reference target improves by 3.232 dB over the prior. These are
conditional simulation results, not claims of uniform population gains.

The compact sparse/EM comparator remains better in pooled linear NMSE
(0.00868525 versus 0.00897384) and aggregate BER (0.489159% versus 0.522496%).
The final receiver has 32 worse condition-mean NMSE values than the full
repair, including eight descriptive pointwise intervals excluding zero.
Both timing windows pass the 10.74-ms p90 target, but one receiver call in
the second window takes 22.276 ms. This is not a per-call hardware deadline.
No observed weak-target bit errors is not proof of zero population BER.

The final receiver uses correct supplied noise/reference information and
screened simulated ULA channels/Gaussian fitting pilots. Its algebraic
rank rule rejects the current constant-modulus pilot design. There is no
physical atomic-array, 2D-array or new hardware validation. There is no
matched reproduction of published URformer. The three trained fits share
one training population. The final grid overrepresents high SNR relative
to many deployment distributions. The inherited nonzero-tail table was
exploratory; new recovery confirmation does not certify that older table.
