\section{Separately Trained Depth, Width and Training Controls}
This reduced-budget study answers the depth and capacity questions using
new training, not a prefix of the original network. Each dataset contains
4,096 training and 448 independent validation worlds. Training uses 12
epochs, batch size 32 and Adam with learning rate 0.001. There are seven
SNRs from $-10$ to 20~dB, RSR 0, 5, 12 and 20~dB, and either random
three-to-seven-path or fixed two-path channels. This gives 56 conditions,
with $N=32,K=3,P=30$. Unlike the original training mixture, this one
includes RSR 0~dB. Its scores must not be pooled with the original study.

Seven variants share dataset zero and three initialization seeds: the
ten-stage, width-16 baseline; depths five and twenty; widths eight and
32; two experts; and curriculum training. The baseline also trains on
two additional independent datasets with two initialization seeds each,
giving 25 jobs and 300 completed epochs. Initialization seeds do not
create independent training populations. Training has no channel targets.
Validation selects the checkpoint using the equal-condition average of
withheld Rician likelihood, with a fixed 21/9 pilot split. The final
epoch is also evaluated, not substituted when its test error is better.

Ordinary variants share example order and pilot masks at each epoch.
Depth changes the number of learned steps and uses $(t+1)/T$ as its
stage input. The two-expert model combines two width-16 controller outputs
with a learned softmax gate driven by the three spectral features. It has
374 parameters, compared with 364 for width 32. The experts start at
relative thresholds 0.05 and 0.2 rather than the plain controller's 0.1.
They do not recover distinct physical channel components. Architecture
and initialization both change; any gain cannot be attributed to gating
alone.

The curriculum presents RSR 12/20 examples twice per epoch for six epochs,
then RSR 0/5 examples twice per epoch for six epochs. Total exposure per
example matches ordinary training. The repeated visits share that epoch's
pilot mask, so the number of distinct masks per example differs. This is
not an isolated test of ordering alone. Neither channel truth nor test
errors determine this schedule.

After all checkpoints were fixed, 64 fresh worlds per condition were
generated, giving 3,584 paired test worlds. All 25 selected and 25 final
models use the same observations, together with EM100 and the previously
frozen in-loop OptShrink rule. That rule transfers from its earlier
development mixture; it is not retuned on these new tests. A documented
test-seed amendment was made before generation to avoid overlap with
diagnostic seeds. Training and selection were unchanged.

\subsection{Accuracy and Cost}
The reported macro-average gives each condition and initialization equal
weight in dB. Five stages are faster, but lose 1.977~dB against ten.
Twenty gain 0.181~dB while increasing mean time from 8.04 to 15.60~ms.
Their severe-error rate is also higher: 13.46\% versus 10.28\% in this
56-condition mixture. The depth comparison is therefore not a simple
``more is better'' result. Twenty stages improve the RSR 0~dB average
by 1.547~dB but worsen one low-SNR cell by 1.586~dB. The absolute
severe-error rates use a broader, weaker-reference mixture than the
original experiment and are not evidence of a change in its saved results.

Width eight loses 0.731~dB and has almost unchanged processing time:
matrix operations, not controller size, dominate this implementation.
Width 32 gains 0.065~dB on average and reduces severe errors, but loses
0.970~dB in its worst cell. The two-expert gain is only 0.023~dB overall
and 0.179~dB for two-path cells; width 32 gains 0.286~dB on those cells.
The experts therefore do not demonstrate a useful advantage over the
similarly sized plain controller in this check.

The curriculum gains 0.206~dB overall and 0.255~dB at RSR 0~dB, but
loses 2.353~dB at SNR 20~dB, RSR 5~dB and two paths. It does not solve
weak-reference or few-path estimation generally. No tested variant
improves every cell. The original primary estimator is retained; these
test results are not used to choose a replacement.

Timing uses one CPU thread, double precision and batch size one, includes
initialization, and repeats 32 shared SNR 5~dB/RSR 12~dB worlds three
times in shuffled model order. Means average individual-model inference,
not ensemble inference. This timing batch is separate from the original
and OptShrink timing batches; cross-batch speed ratios are not claimed.
The 25-job training invocation took about 103 minutes on the local CPU,
including data preparation and validation, but excluding fresh testing.

\subsection{Variability and Scope}
The three independent training datasets give baseline two-initialization
macro-averages of $-12.450$, $-12.124$ and $-12.229$~dB. Their individual
initialization scores are $(-12.391,-12.509)$, $(-11.824,-12.425)$ and
$(-12.143,-12.315)$~dB. These few replicates expose variability but do not
estimate broad population uncertainty reliably. All use the same test
worlds; repeated predictions are not independent channel realizations.

The paired intervals resample 64 whole cases within each condition 2,000
times, retaining paired methods and averaging the three fixed-model scores.
They are exploratory, conditional and unadjusted across six comparisons.
Small intervals do not remove uncertainty from training or prove an
architecture optimum. Most selected checkpoints are the final epoch;
12 epochs do not establish convergence.

The transferred OptShrink rule gives $-12.150$~dB and 12.11\% severe
errors; EM100 gives $-8.784$~dB and 25.53\%. The new baseline's
$-12.390$~dB score cannot reverse the earlier controlled OptShrink result:
both the training mixture and test weighting differ, and the classical
settings were not developed for this mixture. These are transfer controls,
not a new claim of tuned-classical superiority.

\subsection{Saved-Result Audit}
All 25 histories, 300 epochs, 50 checkpoint hashes and 2,912 prediction
files were checked. The latter comprise 1,400 selected-model, 1,400
final-model and 112 classical files. Errors, severe-error rates and
headline averages were recalculated from predictions and truth. Matching
orders and masks were checked with the stated curriculum exception.
One world per test condition was regenerated from its seed; selected
and final models were replayed individually on a saved case. The largest
replay difference was below $1.2\times10^{-15}$. Seventeen focused tests
cover the new numerical/training/seed checks and OptShrink implementation.
The release retains these audit tools and saved observations, not just
summary scores. This checks the reported computation; it is not external
replication or real-receiver validation.
