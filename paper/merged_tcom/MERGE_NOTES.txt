WHAT CHANGED

One question now connects the two papers: when and how should Hankel structure
be applied to referenced magnitude-only channel estimation, and does learning
add value over properly tuned classical processing?

The repeated introductions, observation models, Rician derivations and Hankel
explanations are combined. P1's classical findings motivate the learned method.
P2's self-supervised training and stronger negative controls complete the story.
The abstract stays qualitative. No uniform-superiority, first-of-its-kind,
state-of-the-art, convergence or hardware-performance claim is added.
The unpublished companion-paper citation is removed because its relevant
content is now part of this manuscript.

Sections I-II: unified scope, prior work, effective receiver/channel model,
local information and the geometric reference. The key local recovery and
CCRB qualifications remain. The tangent construction is given in the main text.

Section III: exact EM and gradient relation, context-only spectral initializer,
hard Hankel processing, rank selection, empirical smooth filtering and the
stronger OptShrink adaptation. Distinct names identify HS-amplitude (P1 and
original P2) and HS-Rician (fresh P2 stronger-baseline study).

Section IV: both learned cores, all eight observable inputs, exact parameter
count, matrix-solve shrinkage, self-supervised masking and validation, and
limited mathematical properties. Important P2 supplement details are brought
into the main manuscript rather than left essential to an external supplement.

Sections V-VI: separate campaign settings, paired aggregation definitions,
classical array/effective-rank results, harmful projection and sweep sensitivity,
learned ablations, the fresh stronger classical comparison, weak-reference and
few-path losses, position mismatch, tight-pilot and nearly noiseless failures.
There is no summed headline trial count that would double-count reused worlds.
The original 64-fold speed ratio is limited to the original rank-search control.
The stronger control has comparable measured time, not that speed ratio.

Section VII: scoped interpretation, actual computational cost, limitations and
one conclusion. The key claim is a measured accuracy/failure/computation trade-off.

IMPORTANT REMAINING SCIENTIFIC LIMITS
1. One-sweep hard processing improves P1's original four-sweep baseline, but
   has not been evaluated on the fresh 1,152-world P2 OptShrink test set.
   The merged manuscript states this and does not claim superiority over all HS
   choices. A targeted, predeclared paired comparison is the most useful next
   numerical addition if a stronger superiority claim is desired.
2. No matched URformer or angle-domain sparse phase-retrieval benchmark exists
   in these combined experiments. The paper does not claim to beat them.
3. In-loop OptShrink has 0.143 dB lower primary average NMSE; the learned model
   has fewer severe errors. These are different performance criteria. The
   negative result must remain visible.
4. All evidence is simulation-based with an effective receiver model. Learned
   intervals condition on fixed models; weak reference, very low noise and
   calibration uncertainty are not resolved by this merge.
5. Combining papers improves the argument and breadth, not novelty by itself.
   The existing ingredients are acknowledged as established. TCOM's judgment
   of significance and novelty remains an editorial/reviewer decision.
