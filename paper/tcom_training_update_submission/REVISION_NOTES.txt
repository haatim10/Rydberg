COMBINED TCOM TRAINING UPDATE - 15 OCTOBER 2026

The two unpublished studies form one journal manuscript. The original
188-parameter estimator remains the main learned method. The paper asks
when structural filtering helps and what accuracy-cost trade-off a small
self-supervised controller can provide. It does not claim that this
estimator is always more accurate than OptShrink.

WHAT THIS UPDATE ADDS
- The full matched all-pilot comparison: 8,192 fresh physical worlds,
  including 8,064 primary and 128 separately reported stress worlds.
  Three matched seeds use 16,384 training examples and 20 epochs each.
- Final-epoch sensitivity results, kept separate from the primary
  validation-selected checkpoint comparison.
- A separate one-seed 50/50 mixed-training screen on 4,096 fresh worlds,
  including 4,032 primary cases. Its failed screening criteria are retained.
- A separate one-seed selector screen on 4,224 fresh physical worlds,
  including 4,032 primary cases. The same worlds are reused with correct
  and +/-3 dB supplied noise variances; this is not three independent sets.
- Simple-English explanations of the accuracy/failure trade-off and the
  remaining sensitivity to wrong noise inputs. Negative follow-ups are
  not promoted as successful improvements or new contributions.
- Generated tables, a training comparison figure and compact evidence
  records. Earlier protocols and their results remain separately labelled.

LATEST MATCHED TRAINING COMPARISON
Primary metric: equal-weight average of per-condition dB NMSE; lower is
better. Severe error means channel NMSE above 1, not communication outage.
All values below come from the same 8,064 primary worlds:

Method                         NMSE (dB)    Severe rate     CPU (ms)
Original estimator              -13.6843        2.819%        4.808
All-pilot training              -13.9616        4.473%        4.811
Offline-tuned OptShrink          -13.1684        4.043%       15.658
Per-block rank-selected OptShrink -14.1321       6.758%      108.078

All-pilot training improves the average by 0.2773 dB over the original,
but increases severe errors. It remains 0.1705 dB behind per-block
rank-selected OptShrink on that average. Final-epoch all-pilot versus
original improvement is 0.2423 dB. The architecture and inference cost are
unchanged; training noise, pilot geometry and objective change together.
The main method is not replaced based on this trade-off.

SMALL DEVELOPMENT FOLLOW-UPS
Mixed training: 0.1520 dB better average than the original in its own fresh
bank, with severe errors 3.745% versus 2.902%. It reduces the all-pilot
severe rate of 4.762%, but does not meet all screening criteria. Its runtime
is 5.008 ms versus 4.980 ms for the original in that timing run.

Selector: observation-only validation selects the constant original-model
policy. It gives zero accuracy improvement, with only small dispatch
overhead. This does not prove that every possible selector would fail.
The 21/9 held-out-pilot selection criterion may favour the original recipe.

On the selector screen's 4,032 primary worlds, original/all-pilot severe
rates are 2.803%/4.861% with correct variance, 11.111%/14.509% when supplied
variance is 3 dB too low, and 3.398%/5.432% when it is 3 dB too high.
Actual observations and physical noise are unchanged across these arms.
These are development stress tests, not proof of calibration robustness.

WHAT REMAINS FROM THE EARLIER COMPACT STUDY
That separate 4,096-world comparison found 1.967 dB average improvement
over the tested 148-parameter NA-ALISTA-inspired adaptation. It is not a
reproduction of published NA-ALISTA, nor proof that a larger search or
longer training would not improve the comparator. Its results are not
pooled with the newer training comparison.

CLAIMS AND LIMITS
- The contribution is a specific self-supervised controller for spatial
  filtering within the Rician channel update, plus measured operating
  limits. Hankel filtering and compact adaptive unfolding are not new by
  themselves. No first-ever, universal superiority or optimality claim.
- Timing comparisons are measured CPU implementation results, including
  initialization and per-block rank selection/refitting where applicable.
  They exclude offline training/tuning and are not hardware guarantees.
- Three training seeds share one dataset. Conditional paired intervals do
  not establish generalization across independent training populations.
- The mixed and selector screens use one seed and are exploratory. Their
  test banks are not untouched confirmation data for a revised method.
- No full-estimator convergence or shrinkage-optimality guarantee.
- Weak references, nearly noiseless observations and wrong noise inputs
  remain limitations. No hardware, atomic-dynamics or 2D-array validation.
- The all-pilot study changes several training factors together. It does
  not isolate pilot count alone or remove the known-noise assumption.
- No matched reproduction of published URformer or full LRHD/HVaF/LRPR.
