\section{Controlled In-Loop OptShrink Comparison}
This experiment uses the frozen original three EM models. A separate
development set has 32 worlds at each of seven SNRs. For both in-loop and
final-only OptShrink, the candidate grid includes ranks 1--15, mixing
0.25, 0.5 or 1, update budgets 10, 20, 50 or 100, and no filtering.
Candidates fit 21 pilots; the other nine score Rician likelihood.
Noise-dependent settings are frozen before generating 128 fresh worlds
at each SNR and at each of two stress settings, giving 1,152 test worlds.
All test methods use all 30 pilots. Reference strength is 12~dB and
channels have three to seven paths, except in the labelled stress cells.

Unlike the first final-filter diagnostic, this control shrinks the lifted
channel after each EM update and averages its antidiagonals. It is an
OptShrink-based Rician reconstruction, not full LRHD on directly observed
noisy Hankel matrices. Hankel lifting and nonlinear reconstruction produce
dependent errors, so matrix-denoising optimality guarantees are not claimed.
Both noise-dependent and global settings are retained in the results.

The retained narrow-population figure includes these saved results
and the added one-sweep comparison. The per-SNR intervals are conditional
paired-case intervals, with no per-SNR multiplicity adjustment.

The selected in-loop control uses ten iterations up to SNR 10~dB and
twenty at 15 and 20~dB. The learned model loses 0.143~dB on the declared
seven-SNR average. It gains 0.723~dB at $-10$~dB but loses 0.796~dB at
20~dB. Its severe-error rate is 0.89\%, versus 5.02\% for the selected
in-loop method. At $-10$~dB these rates are 6.25\% and 35.16\%; the
other primary SNR cells have none in this sample. The lower mean per-case
linear error of the learned method is a different metric, not a reason
to replace the original primary metric after seeing the results.

At RSR 0~dB and SNR 5~dB, the selected in-loop control has NMSE
$-5.026$~dB, versus $-7.069$~dB for learned and $-8.307$~dB for HS-Rician.
However, its 50- and 100-iteration controls reach $-8.790$ and $-8.981$~dB.
The apparent learned advantage over the selected control therefore does
not establish superiority over the in-loop family at weak references.
For two paths, HS-Rician reaches $-17.833$~dB, learned $-16.814$~dB and
selected in-loop $-16.407$~dB. No algorithm wins every comparison.

End-to-end single-thread CPU timing at SNR 5~dB includes initialization,
noise-level lookup and the selected reconstruction. Mean times are 5.67~ms
for one learned model, averaged across three trained models, and 6.20~ms
for selected in-loop OptShrink. Final-only filtering takes 3.99~ms and
HS-Rician 356.65~ms in this timing sample. These are not ensemble inference
times, embedded-device measurements or GPU benchmarks. The original
64-fold ratio applies only to the earlier HS-GS comparison. Saved outputs
were recalculated; independent OptShrink primitive replays agree within
$5\times10^{-15}$ and selected learned replays agree exactly.

\section{Static Noise and Reference Estimation}
This exploratory check estimates a single positive reference-amplitude
multiplier $a$ shared by the array and a shared complex noise variance
$v$. Reference shape and phase remain supplied. It does not estimate
per-antenna drift or choose new measurements. With posterior field $Q$,
let $R=I-S^H(SS^H)^{-1}S$. One conditional least-squares update is
\begin{align}
 a^+&=\operatorname{clip}_{[0.25,4]}
       \frac{\operatorname{Re}\langle BR,QR\rangle_F}{\|BR\|_F^2},\\
 G^+&=(Q-a^+B)S^H(SS^H)^{-1},\\
 v^+&=\operatorname{clip}_{[10^{-6},10^3]}
       \operatorname{mean}\{Z^2+|M^+|^2-2\operatorname{Re}[(M^+)^*Q]\},
\end{align}
where $M^+=G^+S+a^+B$. Bounds use the normalized simulation units and
are algorithm assumptions, not measured receiver limits. If $BR=0$,
the gain is confounded with the channel and the joint fit is rejected.
This denominator check is not a global identifiability proof for the
full nonlinear problem.

Three choices---unchanged inputs, noise estimation, and joint gain/noise
estimation---are fitted on 21 pilots for 5, 20 or 100 iterations.
The other nine choose a setting, which is refitted on all 30 observations.
The selection loss includes $\log v$:
\begin{equation}
 \ell_{\rm nuisance}=(z-|\mu|)^2/v-\log I_{0e}(2z|\mu|/v)+\log v.
\end{equation}
Only $-\log(2z)$ may be omitted when comparing different variances.
All nine candidates and the final refit count toward processing cost.
The calibration preprocessing averages 45.05~ms per case in this CPU
check, before the unchanged learned estimator runs. It is not a free
robustness improvement.
An EM control with the same held-out stopping choice separates stopping
effects from nuisance estimation. Corrected inputs are also passed to
the unchanged learned model; there is no retraining on these cases.

There are 32 worlds per SNR/reference cell, at SNR $-5,5,15$~dB and
RSR 0 or 12~dB: 192 independent worlds. Seven assumed-input conditions
reuse them, giving 1,344 condition-cases, not independent worlds.
The conditions are correct inputs, variance errors of $\pm6$~dB,
reference-amplitude errors of $\pm10\%$, joint $+10\%/+6$~dB error,
and constant phase error $+10^\circ$.

Average improvement does not make the correction safe in every cell.
With weak reference at SNR $-5$~dB and a $+6$~dB variance error, it
worsens learned NMSE by 1.048~dB. At SNR 15~dB with strong reference,
a $+10\%$ amplitude error is largely corrected: NMSE changes from
$-16.346$ to $-24.107$~dB; the true-input diagnostic is $-24.253$~dB.
These are simulation input errors, not observed hardware behavior.
Unknown constant phase remains unresolved because channel and reference
can rotate together. Likelihood improvement cannot certify absolute phase
or reduced channel error. The original known-calibration estimator remains
the primary method; this check is not a universal calibration remedy.

The new pilot sweep has $P=6,8,10,12,20,30$, $K=3$, SNR $-5,5,15$~dB,
RSR 12~dB and 32 fresh worlds per cell, or 576 worlds. Learned weights
and OptShrink settings are transferred from $P=30$, without tuning on
these tests. HS-Rician fits ranks on $\operatorname{round}(0.7P)$ pilots,
scores the remainder, then refits all $P$. Its rank-fitting subset can
be below $2K$ even when $P$ reaches it. Structural coupling across array
elements means the unrestricted single-row count is not, by itself, an
identifiability theorem for the structurally constrained model.

At SNR 5~dB, learned NMSE is $-2.197$~dB with six pilots and
$-14.332$~dB with thirty. At SNR $-5$~dB and six pilots it is
$4.089$~dB, showing substantial failure. All sampled nonzero-reference
full-data row Jacobians have rank six. Nevertheless, at SNR 5~dB the
median smallest singular value is 0.172 with six pilots and 2.488 with
thirty. Local full rank is not enough for stable finite-noise estimation
or global recovery. The cells use different independent worlds; the
method comparisons within each cell are paired.

A second check shares the same channel, pilots and complex noise across
absent reference and RSR $-10,0,5,12$~dB, at SNR 5, 20 and 60~dB.
There are 32 independent worlds per SNR and 480 condition-cases.
The SNR 5~dB reference check shares a generation seed and channels with
one tight-pilot cell. The two diagnostic phases are not independent
replications of each other and are not pooled for uncertainty estimates.
At zero reference, sampled Jacobians have rank five and absolute phase
is ambiguous. At nonzero reference they have rank six in this sample.
At SNR 20~dB and RSR $-10$~dB, ordinary EM gives $-1.773$~dB NMSE
from spectral initialization but $-21.036$~dB when initialized at truth.
The latter is an oracle diagnostic only. The gap supports an initialization
or optimization difficulty; it does not prove global identifiability or
exclude controller problems.

\subsection{A Nearly Noiseless Failure}
SNR 60~dB lies far outside the training range. Even at RSR 12~dB the
frozen learned model gives $-6.647$~dB, while EM100 gives $-65.715$~dB.
A post-hoc intervention on the same 32 cases disables only learned
filtering and retains the learned step sizes; its NMSE is $-44.020$~dB.
Starting the full learned model at the true channel still gives
$-6.653$~dB. Thus filtering can spoil an already accurate estimate in
this setting; the failure is not explained solely by a bad initializer.
Independent NumPy arithmetic for the no-filter intervention agrees with
the PyTorch result within $5\times10^{-15}$. This is an out-of-range
failure and a post-hoc diagnostic, not an independently confirmed switching
policy. No low-noise consistency or generally safe filtering claim is made.
