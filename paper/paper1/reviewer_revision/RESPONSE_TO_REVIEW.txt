RESPONSE TO THE PAPER 1 REVIEW
Prepared for the author, 8 October 2026. Nothing has been sent to the reviewer.
Status: supporting experiments completed; main and budget audits passed.
The following describes prepared revisions, not an already integrated PDF.

Thank you for the review. The additional comparisons support a more precise
conclusion: the usefulness of Hankel processing depends on both the channel
and the chosen structural operation. Smooth shrinkage can outperform hard
rank truncation. We therefore narrow the conclusions about HS-GS instead of
claiming that its observed boundary applies to all Hankel methods.

The new main campaign contains 12,000 condition-trial evaluations from 4,800
base worlds; split fractions and mismatch strengths share those base worlds.
It passed the independent completeness, provenance, pairing and numerical
replay audit. The 2,800 RSR worlds are reused from a separate audited campaign,
and the 10,400 EM-only replays reuse existing Paper 1 worlds. Neither is added
to the fresh-world count. A separate budget check uses 300 new worlds; serial
timing uses 20 worlds. The original Paper 1 results remain unchanged.

1. Stronger structured comparisons and shrinkage

We added exact-Rician gradient descent with hard Hankel processing, empirical
smooth shrinkage inside EM-GS, unstructured Rician gradient descent and Xu's
one-dimensional linearized least-squares estimator. All receive the same exact
magnitude observations; iterative methods share spectral initialization.
Each structured method chooses its own parameter from the held-out residual
and then refits all pilots, using 25 candidate and 100 final updates.

Smooth shrinkage improves on HS-GS in five of six tested cells by 0.623--1.413
dB, with pointwise 95% intervals above zero. The sixth difference is 0.005 dB
[-0.180,0.191], which does not establish equivalence. At L=14/SNR=0 dB, HS-GS
loses 0.147 dB against EM-GS, whereas smooth shrinkage gains 1.266 dB. Thus,
the hard-HS failure region is not a universal limit of Hankel information.

This is an empirical smooth rule, not OptShrink or a nuclear-norm estimator.
The projected-gradient control adapts the Rician model to our per-user Hankel
lift; it is not a reproduction of Xu's physical two-dimensional projection.
We clarify that hard truncation leaves retained observed singular values
unchanged and does not correct their noise-induced bias.

2. Reference strength

At N=32, K=3, P=30 and SNR=5 dB, we reanalyse 400 paired trials at each RSR
of 0,5,10,12,15,20,25 dB. Only classical EM-GS/HS-GS results are used.
Ratio-of-sums gains range from 1.83 to 2.51 dB, all with positive pointwise
95% intervals. At RSR=0, the gain is 1.83 dB [1.40,2.26]. Both methods still
suffer with the weak reference: EM/HS NMSE is -6.19/-8.01 dB there, versus
-10.73/-12.93 dB at RSR=12. No monotonic or universal RSR claim is made.

3. Held-out fraction, pilot overhead and runtime

The final fit uses all pilots; the temporary split does not permanently discard
pilots or require additional transmissions. It reduces each candidate's fitting
data and adds repeated computation. We tested 10%,20%,30%,40% at P=10,30 and
SNR=0,10 dB, sharing worlds across fractions. The best point estimate changes
with the setting. At P10/SNR10, 40% is worse than 30% by 0.461 dB
[0.176,0.744]. At P30/SNR10, 10% is worse by 0.602 dB [0.383,0.832]. Larger
validation sets tend to select smaller ranks. We do not claim 30% is optimal.

Separate serial timing gives median totals of 348.5 ms for HS-GS, 346.5 ms
for Rician GD plus Hankel, and 200.8 ms for smooth shrinkage. Median selection
shares are 76.0%,75.9%,56.4%. These are 20 CPU worlds at N32/P30/L7/SNR5,
with one requested thread. Shrinkage searches seven factors versus 16 ranks;
this is not an equal-search-budget speed comparison. Machine, fixed method
order and implementation limit the timing claim.

4. Observable effective rank

We replayed EM-GS on 10,400 existing worlds. Every reproduced EM error equals
its stored value in this implementation. Measuring rank before HS processing
avoids a correlation imposed by the projection itself. At N32/P30, estimated-
versus-true rank Spearman correlation rises from 0.202 [0.100,0.297] at -5 dB
to 0.914 [0.890,0.931] at 20 dB.

Noise raises the proxy: at 5 dB its mean is 0.577 versus a true mean of 0.244,
while the paired-median gain is 2.544 dB. These rank means average users
within trials; the original plotted rho uses operating-point medians. This
comparison of averages does not test a per-trial cutoff. Within fixed 5 dB
SNR bands across complexity, proxy--gain correlation ranges from -0.098
[-0.183,-0.012] to -0.797 [-0.836,-0.752]. Whole-trial bootstrap intervals
preserve the pairing. The proxy has conditional value, but requires calibration
before serving as a rank-selection rule. Normalized true path count is also
reported as an evaluation-only comparator.

5. Mild model mismatch

The position-error study uses Gaussian element offsets with standard deviations
up to 0.02 wavelengths; the realized offsets are not bounded by that value.
Paths, pilots and noise are paired, the reference is calibrated, and the actual
perturbed channel is the target. At L=3, HS gain falls from 4.955 to 3.917 dB. At L=12,
it falls from 0.264 to 0.050 dB; the final interval [-0.029,0.125] does not
establish a positive benefit. The paired losses of gain are 1.038 dB
[0.898,1.183] and 0.213 dB [0.139,0.287], respectively. These stress levels
are not measured hardware tolerances. Near-field propagation, mutual coupling,
correlated noise and an uncalibrated reference remain outside this test.

A separate reused +/-3 dB assumed-variance check changes EM and HS NMSE over
0.040 and 0.043 dB, respectively, at one reference setting. Variance sensitivity
is not evidence about correlated noise or spatial mismatch.

6. Hankelization and fallback

The revised algorithm box states that the lift is per user column, while one
scalar rank is selected jointly and shared across users. No stacked/mosaic
lift or shared-angle model is assumed. Full-capacity selection gives the paired
EM-GS estimate exactly. It is an available fallback, not a per-trial no-harm
guarantee. Finite Cadzow sweeps need not end at exact rank r after averaging.

7. CCRB appendix

The prepared appendix gives the real coordinate ordering, gains/angles,
channel Jacobian, orthonormal tangent basis, Fisher projection and plotted
normalization. Gains and angles are local channel parameters; noise variance
and the reference remain known. Unknown calibration is not added as a nuisance
parameter. The CRLB/CCRB are local unbiased references, and their gap is not a
guaranteed attainable gain for the biased, rank-selected estimators.

8. Update budget

The separate 300-world check continues the same five iterative methods from
100 to 300 updates with selected parameters fixed. Every cell-level NMSE point
change is below 0.06 dB in magnitude. Some estimates still change at 300 updates,
and lower likelihood loss does not guarantee lower channel error. This supports
limited observed budget sensitivity, not equivalence or global convergence.

9. Scope and remaining work

Related-work fragments acknowledge classical structured low-rank recovery,
spectral compressed sensing, MRI Hankel models and OptShrink. We do not claim
a new general recovery principle, universal rho threshold, or superiority to
all structured estimators. OptShrink itself, nuclear-norm recovery, tensor or
shared-user models, and AIC/MDL/SURE selectors were not tested.
