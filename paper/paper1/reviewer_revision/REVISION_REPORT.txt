PAPER 1 REVISION REPORT
8 October 2026. Prepared for the author; nothing has been sent to the reviewer.
Status: main experiment and budget-check audits passed; serial timing complete.

The reviewer response now has stronger evidence, but the new results require
a narrower conclusion. HS-GS is useful in many tested conditions; it is not
the best tested structural method. Smooth shrinkage beats hard-rank HS-GS
in five of six baseline cells, including a complex channel where HS-GS hurts.
The original paper should therefore describe when its particular hard-rank
procedure helps, rather than a universal limit of Hankel priors.

STRUCTURED COMPARISONS

Settings: N32/K3/P30/RSR12; L=3,7,14 and SNR=0,10 dB; 400 paired trials/cell.
All estimators use the same exact magnitude observations. Iterative methods
share spectral initialization and 100 final updates. Each structural method
chooses its own rank or threshold with 25 context updates, held-out amplitude
residual and an all-pilot final refit. Four structural sweeps are used.

Gain is ratio-of-sums NMSE improvement; positive values favor the named method.

Smooth shrinkage has a positive paired interval over HS in five cells; the
remaining interval includes zero and does not establish equivalence. At L14,
SNR0, hard HS is worse than EM, but shrinkage still provides a useful gain.
This is the main change to the paper's interpretation. It does not invalidate
the original hard-HS numbers; it limits how widely they can be generalized.
The new shrinkage comparator has not been verified across every old array,
RSR or alternative-channel experiment.

The unstructured Rician gradient method is close to EM at this budget; adding
Hankel processing generally reproduces the sparsity-related pattern. Xu's
linearized LS comparison is less accurate than EM at all six points, especially
at SNR10, where its NMSE is approximately 3.90--4.14 dB worse. It is a named
linearized comparison on exact observations, not Xu's two-dimensional method.

HELD-OUT FRACTION

Below are HS gains over EM for each fraction, on shared base worlds:

P   SNR     10%      20%      30%      40%
10    0    3.467    3.912    3.987    4.113
10   10    2.630    2.930    2.723    2.262

30   10    1.791    2.257    2.393    2.459

The best point estimate changes with P and SNR. Many differences have intervals
that include zero. At P10/SNR10, 40% is worse than 30% by 0.461 dB
[0.176,0.744]; at P30/SNR10, 10% is worse by 0.602 dB [0.383,0.832]. The mean
selected rank decreases as the held-out fraction increases in all four settings.
For example, at P10/SNR0 it falls from 3.750 to 1.948. Fallback is rare in this
study, ranging from 0% to 1.5%. All final estimates refit all pilots. The results
support reporting sensitivity, not replacing 30% with a universally best split.

wavelengths. These values do not bound the realized Gaussian offsets.
Settings are N32/P30/SNR5/RSR12 with L3 or L12. Paths, pilots,
reference calibration and noise are paired across strengths. The error target
is the actual perturbed channel, without realized-power renormalization.

At the largest tested standard deviation, gain drops by 1.038 dB [0.898,1.183] for L3 and
0.213 dB [0.139,0.287] for L12, using paired differences from zero error.
L3 retains a clear gain; L12's remaining 0.050 dB has interval [-0.029,0.125].
The latter does not establish a positive benefit. These declared stress levels
are not measured hardware tolerances. Calibrated reference, far-field model
and independent noise remain assumptions; broader mismatch was not tested.

RSR AND OBSERVABLE-RANK ADDITIONS

The reused classical RSR gains span 1.827--2.512 dB and stay positive, with
pointwise intervals above zero. Weak reference still worsens both absolute
NMSEs. At RSR0, EM/HS NMSE is -6.19/-8.01 dB versus -10.73/-12.93 dB at RSR12.
This is a reference-strength check at N32/SNR5, not universal robustness.

The raw EM rank proxy becomes more informative at higher SNR, but is noise-
biased. At N32/P30, proxy-to-truth correlation is 0.202 [0.100,0.297] at -5 dB
and 0.914 [0.890,0.931] at 20 dB. At 5 dB its mean is 0.577 versus true 0.244,
while HS paired-median gain is 2.544 dB. Rank means, gain medians and original
operating-point rho medians are distinct summaries. This is not a per-trial
threshold test. A receiver rule would require calibration and fresh validation.
Normalized true path count remains a useful evaluation-only comparator.

At assumed-noise offsets -3/0/+3 dB, absolute EM and HS NMSE span 0.040 and
0.043 dB in the reused reference design. This does not test correlated noise.

BUDGET AND SERIAL TIMING

Continuing five iterative methods from 100 to 300 updates changes each cell's
NMSE point estimate by less than 0.06 dB in magnitude; the largest change is
0.051 dB. Selected parameters are fixed after 25 candidate updates. This is
limited budget sensitivity, not a claim of equivalence, optimum performance
or complete numerical convergence. Some trajectories still change at 300.

Serial CPU timings at N32/K3/P30/L7/SNR5/RSR12, 20 worlds, requested one thread:

Method                    Median total ms   Median selection share
HS-GS                          348.5               76.0%
Rician GD + Hankel              346.5               75.9%
EM-GS + smooth shrinkage        200.8               56.4%
EM-GS                            9.94               0%
Rician GD                       10.01               0%
Xu 1D linearized LS               0.65               0%

Total time includes selection and final-fit initialization, but excludes data
generation. Shrinkage searches seven threshold factors versus 16 hard ranks;
the timing is not an equal-search-budget comparison. Methods ran serially in
fixed disclosed order, with no discarded warmup world. These are measurements
of one implementation and machine, not portable complexity constants. Parallel
Monte Carlo workload times are not used as latency benchmarks.

Publication assessment: this is a stronger empirical paper, provided the
contribution is narrowed honestly. Smooth shrinkage's success prevents claims
that hard HS is best or that its failure boundary applies to all Hankel priors.
It does not establish a new universally superior shrinkage estimator either.
OptShrink itself, nuclear-norm methods, tensor/shared-user models, AIC/MDL/SURE
selectors, and further mismatch families remain optional untested extensions.
