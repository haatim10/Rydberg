Must fix, and what to run next

20.1 MUST FIX BEFORE PROFESSOR REVIEW — ordered by seriousness

Ordered by how badly each would damage the work if raised by a reviewer before you raised it.

1 — critical · scope

The frozen technical report still carries the defective CRLB

The corrected bound (rank-1 per magnitude measurement, §10.3) and the constrained bound (orthonormal tangent basis, §10.7) exist as constrained_crlb_fast.py and constrained_crlb.json, and every figure and table in this artifact uses them. TECHNICAL_REPORT.md and any figure generated before the correction do not. The two disagree by up to 4.19 dB. Any reviewer comparing the two documents will find the discrepancy. Action: regenerate or explicitly supersede the frozen report's CRLB sections before circulating either.

2 — critical · over-claim risk

No result here supports a general claim about RAQR channel estimation

Every Track-B number was produced under an i.i.d. uniform-AoA, equal-power, flat, narrowband geometric model. The defensible claim is "under a sparse geometric multipath model, exploiting Hankel low-rank structure yields up to 2.85 dB over EM-GS at N = 32." The claim "HS-GS improves RAQR channel estimation" is not supported. Action: fix the scope sentence in the abstract, the report, and any slide, before review — not after.

3 — major · presentation

The N = 8 result must never be quoted as a single number

It is −0.19 dB per-operating-point-averaged and +0.81 dB energy-pooled — opposite signs from the same data (§16.2, Q4). Both are correct; the disagreement is the finding. A reviewer who computes the other one will conclude the number was chosen. Action: wherever N = 8 is summarised, quote both with the one-line explanation, or quote the per-point behaviour ("wins at low SNR, loses at high SNR").

4 — major · disclosure

The shared scalar L̂ must be stated wherever HS-GS is described

A single order is imposed on all K users; the true orders were (7, 4, 6) and 5 was selected. This is disclosed in §9.6 and Q7 here, but it is the kind of thing that reads as concealment if a reviewer discovers it in the code first. Action: put it in the method description, not only in the limitations.

5 — major · correctness of presentation

The P = 6 CRLB of +46.87 dB must never appear unexplained

It is correct — 48 unknowns, 48 rank-1 measurements, a near-singular Fisher matrix — and it is useless as a bound. On a plot it looks like a bug. Action: either annotate it as a degenerate point or exclude P = 6 from CRLB overlays, and say which was done (§16.5).

6 — moderate · missing sources

Two of the five input papers were never supplied

The Xu paper and the Xiao/URformer paper are absent from the uploads. Parts VIII and XV are written from implementation and first principles and are labelled NOT VERIFIED throughout. Action: supply them, or remove every comparative statement that references them. Do not cite either paper's results on the strength of this document.

7 — moderate · reproducibility

tex/formulae.tex has never been compiled

1,197 lines, 57 equations, structurally validated but no TeX distribution exists in this environment. Action: compile it once somewhere that has LaTeX before shipping it as a reference.

8 — moderate · theory gap

HS-GS has no convergence result of any kind

Non-convex constraint set, approximate (4-sweep Cadzow) projection, no merit function, no descent guarantee. Action: state this in the method section as a known gap. Do not describe the iteration as "converged"; describe it as run to a fixed 50-iteration budget.

20.2 Recommended next experiments — ranked by scientific value

Ranked by how much each would change what is known, not by how many figures it would produce. Three are listed. Experiments that would only add plots are deliberately excluded, and §20.3 says which those are.

rank 1 — could falsify the result

Run B3 under the Track-A 3GPP TR 38.901 clustered generator

Why it matters most: it is the only proposed experiment that can overturn the work. Under clustering the Hankel matrix is numerically low rank rather than exactly low rank (§14.3), and Cadzow's hard truncation — which is exactly right for an exact rank cliff — becomes a bias-inducing approximation. The N = 8 high-SNR failure mode (Q18) is the warning sign that this could get much worse. Cost: low. The 38.901 generator already exists in Track A and is validated; only the channel call in track_b_spec changes. No new estimator, no new mathematics. Decision rule stated in advance: if the N = 32 gain drops below roughly +1 dB, the scope claim in §20.1(2) must narrow from "sparse geometric multipath" to a statement about resolvable-path channels specifically.

rank 2 — targets the largest known deficiency

Per-user model-order selection

Why: the shared scalar L̂ is the single most obviously wrong thing about the estimator (Q7). The selector is already column-agnostic; making it per-user is a change to the selector, not to the estimator or its validated mathematics. What it would settle: how much of the 0.3–8.9 dB gap to the constrained CRLB (§16.3) is attributable to order misspecification rather than to the projection itself — the gap decomposition is currently unknown, and this is the cheapest way to split it. Cost: moderate; it multiplies selection cost by K, and the per-user selector is noisier since it sees one column instead of K.

rank 3 — isolates the mechanism

Ablation: exact_step = "gs" and project_every ∈ {1, 5, 50} across the full grid

Why: §19.2 lists "the gain comes from the projection, not the EM step" as only partially established — inferred from the RSR sweep rather than measured. Two ablations settle it directly: swapping the exact step to plain GS isolates EM's contribution, and setting project_every = 50 reduces HS-GS to post-hoc Cadzow denoising, which is precisely the "this is just denoising" objection (Q11). Answering that objection with data rather than argument is worth one grid. Cost: low — no new code, just two configurations of the existing driver. Caveat: this is an ablation of the proposed method, not a hyperparameter search; the reported configuration stays fixed regardless of the outcome.

20.3 Experiments deliberately not recommended

  • More SNR points, more N values, finer RSR grids. The trends are established and monotone; denser grids would add figures and no knowledge.
  • More Monte Carlo on already-decided points. The five ambiguous points were already extended to 1,200 trials. Extending points whose CIs are far from zero buys nothing.
  • Hyperparameter sweeps over cadzow_iter, select_iter, val_frac. These were fixed at their initial values and never tuned, which is what makes the comparison against untuned baselines fair. Tuning them now would convert a clean comparison into a contaminated one, and would improve the reported gain for the wrong reason.
  • A fourth estimator. Out of scope by instruction, and the constrained CRLB already bounds what any estimator could achieve on this model — which is the more informative answer.
