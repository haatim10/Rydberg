16.4 Crossings of the CRLB — the full count, and why they happen

This is the result most likely to be challenged, so it is reported in full

Estimators in this study go below the unconstrained CRLB at 63 of the 108 (estimator, operating-point) combinations in B3. This is not a handful of borderline cases and it is not presented as one. The largest excursion is 6.94 dB (HS-GS, N=32, P=10, SNR = −5 dB).

A CRLB bounds the covariance of an unbiased estimator. None of the three estimators here is unbiased, and the direction of their bias is known and is towards zero. Writing the shrinkage coefficient

c = ⟨Ĝ, G⟩ / ‖G‖²(40)

the measured values are c = 0.756 at SNR = −5 dB and c = 0.957 at SNR = +5 dB. An estimator that shrinks trades variance for bias; a sufficiently shrunk estimator has smaller mean-squared error than any unbiased estimator can achieve, and the CRLB simply does not apply to it. This is textbook (James–Stein is the canonical example) and it fully accounts for the pattern in the data:

  • The crossings are largest at low SNR and shrink at high SNR, exactly tracking c → 1. At SNR = −5 dB, GS/EM-GS are 2.28–2.62 dB below the bound at every N; by SNR = 20 dB with P = 30 the same estimators are below by 0.006–0.049 dB, i.e. numerically at the bound.
  • HS-GS crosses by more than the baselines, and by more at larger N (up to 6.94 dB at N=32). Cadzow's hard rank truncation is an additional, deliberate shrinkage: discarding singular values below the L̂-th removes energy. More shrinkage, larger permitted excursion.
  • At high SNR with P = 30 the baselines sit essentially exactly on the bound (0.006–0.049 dB below, within Monte Carlo error at 400 trials). EM-GS is close to efficient for the unstructured problem in that regime. That agreement is what makes the corrected bound credible.
What was done about it, and what was deliberately not done

The bound plotted in Figure B2 is the corrected one. The originally shipped unconstrained CRLB had a genuine defect — it treated each magnitude measurement as contributing rank-2 Fisher information when eq. (29) depends on λ only through |λ| and therefore contributes rank 1 (§10.3) — and disagreed with the corrected bound by up to 4.19 dB. It was found by an independent check (the genie-ZF gap read 3.54 dB where theory demands 3.0103 dB) and confirmed two ways: a direct rank test on the Fisher blocks, and an empirical ML simulation. The defective bound is described in the text but is never plotted. No estimator was changed, no parameter was tuned, and no crossing was hidden by rescaling an axis.

Worth stating explicitly: the correction moved the bound up (rank-1 credits each measurement with less information than rank-2 did), so it increased the number of apparent crossings. The defective bound would have made this document look better. It was replaced anyway.

This supersedes a line in the frozen audit. results/track_b/final_audit.json records the check "EM-GS never falls below the unconstrained CRLB — 0/36 violations", and that was true against the old bound. Against the corrected bound EM-GS falls below it at 19 of 36 points, by 0.009–2.62 dB. The audit check is not wrong; it is answering the question about a bound that has since been replaced. Any reader comparing the two documents will hit this, so it is stated here rather than left to be discovered.

16.5 A degenerate operating point that must not be quoted flagged

In the B4 pilot sweep, the point P = 6 reports an unconstrained CRLB of +46.87 dB. This is not a typographical error and it is not a bug: at P = 6, K = 3, N = 8 the unconstrained problem has 2NK = 48 real unknowns and NP = 48 magnitude measurements, each contributing rank-1 information. The Fisher matrix is on the edge of singularity and its inverse trace blows up. The bound is correct and useless: it says the unstructured problem is essentially unidentifiable there. Note that the constrained bound at the same point is a perfectly ordinary −1.46 dB, because the structural parameterisation has only ≈45 real parameters and is nowhere near degenerate — which is itself a clean illustration of what the structure buys. The +46.87 dB number should never be quoted as "the CRLB at P = 6" without this explanation.

The EM-GS over GS column is the quantitative justification for operating at RSR = 12 dB rather than the 30 dB used in early scratch testing. It collapses from 1.285 dB at RSR = 0 to 0.002 dB at RSR = 24: as the reference grows, R(κ) → 1, the EM soft phase estimate becomes the hard modulus substitution, and Cui's two algorithms become the same algorithm. Had the study been run at 30 dB, "EM-GS vs GS" would have been an empty comparison and the choice of exact step inside HS-GS would have been unfalsifiable.

Note also that HS-GS's sign is determined by N, not by RSR: it loses at every RSR when N = 8 and wins at every RSR when N = 32. The gain is essentially flat in RSR above 6 dB. Whatever HS-GS is exploiting, it is not reference strength.
