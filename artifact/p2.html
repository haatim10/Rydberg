Research problem

A Rydberg atomic receiver with N sensing elements serves K single-antenna users over a multipath channel. The receiver measures only the magnitude of the total RF field at each element. Given known pilots S and a known reference field B, estimate the complex channel matrix G from the real, non-negative measurement Z. This is biased phase retrieval: nonlinear, non-convex, and not solvable by pilot inversion.

Physical motivation

The atomic transduction chain terminates in an optical readout whose response depends on the RF electric-field amplitude, not its phase (§IV). The phase is therefore destroyed at the sensor. A known reference field is deliberately superposed so that relative phase re-enters the measurement through the cross term of |x+b|²; without it the problem has severe ambiguities (§X.4).

Main system model verified

Z = | GS + B + W |,   W ~ 𝒞𝒩(0, σ²I)(1)

Verified by executing the generator: max|·| deviation between the stored Z and a recomputed |GS+B+W| is exactly 0.00e+00 over 240 worlds spanning all three array sizes. The noise enters before the magnitude, which is what makes the problem hard. No linearised observation is used anywhere on this path: a runtime tripwire replaced the linearised solver with a function that raises and confirmed 0 calls across all three estimators at all three array sizes.

Track B1 — literature baseline

Cui's biased Gerchberg–Saxton (GS) and EM-GS, applied unchanged to this ULA channel while retaining the exact observation (1). No structural prior. These establish what existing magnitude-only machinery achieves here, and everything else is measured against them.

Track B2 — proposed structure-aware extension

A ULA channel column is a sum of Lk spatial exponentials, so its Hankel matrix has rank ≤ Lk. HS-GS interleaves a Cadzow rank-truncation projection with every exact EM-GS measurement update, enforcing that structure without any angle grid and without estimating any angle. The model order is selected from held-out pilot residual — not from oracle Lk.

Main results — only what the simulations support

  • The HS-GS advantage over EM-GS is −0.19 / +0.78 / +2.85 dB at N = 8 / 16 / 32 — the unweighted mean over the twelve operating points at each array size — monotone in N, with the N = 32 confidence interval strictly above the N = 16 interval at 12/12 shared operating points.
  • At N = 8 the method loses at 8 of 12 points, all of them the higher-SNR ones, while winning at low SNR. The sign of any single N = 8 summary therefore depends on the pooling: energy-pooling the same trials gives +0.81 dB. Both numbers are reported in §16.2 rather than one being chosen. The N = 16 and N = 32 results carry no such ambiguity — every pooling agrees in sign there.
  • The mechanism was tested directly and confirmed. In a controlled experiment (B7, 3,200 trials) with path count L fixed rather than drawn, the gain decays strictly monotonically from +7.13 dB at L = 2 to +0.04 dB at L = 14 and −0.12 dB at the Hankel rank cap, while the baselines stay flat. The hypothesis was written into the script before the run.
  • The EM-GS baseline is flat in N to 0.012 dB, so the N-dependence belongs to the structural prior and not to the baseline degrading.
  • Against a correctly constructed constrained CRLB, HS-GS is 0.3–8.9 dB above the bound at 34 of 36 operating points, and the gap grows with N (§X.7, §16.3). At the two remaining points it falls below the bound — as biased estimators are permitted to (§16.4) — and those two are reported, not cropped.

Theoretical benchmark

Two bounds are computed, and they answer different questions. The unconstrained CRLB governs estimators that treat G as 2NK free real parameters — GS and EM-GS. The constrained CRLB governs estimators that know G lies on the 3ΣLk-parameter geometric manifold — the only bound against which HS-GS may fairly be judged. Placing HS-GS against the unconstrained bound is invalid, and §X.7 explains why.

Defect found during this audit, not yet corrected in the frozen report

The CRLB implementation inherited from Track A credits each magnitude measurement with constraining both quadratures — its Fisher contribution has real-equivalent rank 2, where a magnitude measurement yields one real number and must contribute rank 1. It is therefore optimistic by up to 4.2 dB. Two independent tests establish this (§X.3), including a simulation in which the maximum-likelihood estimator converges onto the rank-1 bound (0.990× at 35 dB) while sitting above the old one. Every bound in this document uses the corrected construction. No estimator result changes — the CRLB enters no NMSE, gain, confidence interval or win rate.

2.2 ULA geometry — derivation

A plane wave arriving at angle θ from array broadside reaches element n after an extra path length of (n−1) d sin θ relative to element 1. Converting path length to phase at wavelength λ gives a phase lag of (2π/λ)(n−1) d sin θ, so the per-element phase increment is

ψ = (2πd/λ) sin θ(2)

and at half-wavelength spacing d = λ/2 this reduces to

ψ = π sin θ,   θ ∈ [−π/2, π/2] ⟹ ψ ∈ [−π, π](3)

Physical meaning. θ is the physical angle of arrival measured from broadside. ψ is the electrical angle — the phase step between adjacent sensors. The choice d = λ/2 is what makes the map θ → ψ a bijection onto exactly one period of the complex exponential: any wider spacing folds distinct physical angles onto the same electrical angle (grating lobes / spatial aliasing), and any narrower spacing wastes aperture. Verified in the code: psi = pi * sin(theta) and the drawn θ lie inside [−1.5579, 1.5310] against a permitted [−1.5708, 1.5708].

A worked numerical example

Take N = 4, L = 2, θ₁ = 0 (broadside) and θ₂ = 30° = π/6. Then ψ₁ = π·sin 0 = 0 and ψ₂ = π·sin(π/6) = π/2. With α₁ = 1 and α₂ = j:

g = 1·[1, 1, 1, 1] + j·[1, e−jπ/2, e−jπ, e−j3π/2] = [1+j, 1+1, 1−j, 1−1] = [1+j, 2, 1−j, 0](5)

Two paths, four elements: the broadside path contributes a constant, the 30° path a quarter-turn per element, and their interference produces a channel that varies along the array while living in a 2-dimensional subspace of ℂ⁴. That confinement is precisely what §IX exploits.
