11.4 Randomness and Monte Carlo

The master seed is TRACK_B_MASTER_SEED = 20250820. Each trial's world is generated by a SeedSequence keyed by (master_seed, trial_index, snr, rsr), so:

  • Paired across estimators — within one operating point all three estimators see bit-identical {θ, α, G, S, B, W, Z}. This is what makes the paired bootstrap valid.
  • Independent across operating points — the worlds at SNR = 0 dB and SNR = 5 dB are not the same channel at two noise levels. Common random numbers are provided across estimators, not along the sweep axis. A reader who expects CRN along SNR will misread the point-to-point wiggle as an estimator effect; it is not.
  • Lk comes from a dedicated stream keyed (master_seed, trial, 0x4C4B), independent of the channel/pilot/noise streams.

11.5 What is not fixed by the code not verified

  • Carrier frequency, absolute bandwidth, physical array aperture in metres. The model is written in normalised electrical angle ψ = π sin θ; no carrier is ever instantiated. Any statement of the form "this is a 28 GHz system" would be invented. NOT VERIFIED — NEEDS CONFIRMATION if a physical-units claim is required.
  • Rydberg-specific constants — atomic species, principal quantum number, Rabi frequencies, LO detuning. The receiver enters only through c = 1 and the modulus non-linearity. The simulation is a signal-model abstraction of a RAQR, not an atomic-physics simulation, and should be described that way.
  • βk path loss spread. All users have βk = 1: equal average power, no near-far effect. Unequal-β behaviour is untested.
Part XII

NMSE definition and the statistics reported

The single most common way to make two estimators look different when they are not is to change how per-trial errors are pooled. This section states exactly which pooling the code uses, why, and what the alternatives would have shown.

12.1 Three different quantities, all called "NMSE in dB"

For trial t write the numerator et = ‖Ĝt − Gt‖²F and the denominator dt = ‖Gt‖²F. Three estimators of "the NMSE" exist:

(a) ratio of sums:  10 log10( Σt et / Σt dt )(36)
(b) mean of ratios:  10 log10( (1/T) Σt et/dt )(37)
(c) mean of dB:  (1/T) Σt 10 log10( et/dt )(38)

The code uses (a), the ratio of sums. This is verified directly in scripts/analyze_b3.py:

def pooled_db(num, den): return float(10 * np.log10(num.sum() / den.sum()))code

and the module docstring states it explicitly: "never a mean of per-trial dB values." The dB factor is 10, not 20, throughout — e and d are already energies (squared Frobenius norms), so 10 log10 is the correct power-ratio conversion. Applying 20 log10 would double every dB number in this document.

Why (a) and not (b) or (c)

(a) is the estimator of the ensemble NMSE E‖Ĝ−G‖²/E‖G‖², which is the quantity a CRLB bounds. A CRLB is a bound on a variance, i.e. on an expected squared error, so any comparison against a bound must pool in the squared-error domain. (c) is a bound-incompatible statistic: Jensen's inequality makes it strictly smaller than (a), so an estimator plotted with (c) can appear to cross a CRLB that it does not actually violate. (b) is defensible but weights every trial equally regardless of how much channel energy it carries; because βk = 1 and Lk varies, dt fluctuates trial to trial, and (a) is the version that corresponds to total energy delivered.

12.2 The median is reported, but never as the headline

median_per_trial_db = 10 log₁₀(median(et/dt)) is stored at every point as a robustness diagnostic. It answers a different question — "what does a typical trial look like" — and it is not comparable to a CRLB. It is used in exactly one place with consequence: §12.4, to check that a pooled gain is not driven by outliers.

12.3 Confidence intervals

All intervals are 95% paired bootstrap percentile intervals over 2000 resamples. "Paired" matters: the resample index vector is drawn once and applied to all three estimators, so the interval on the gain

gaindB = 10 log10( Σt eEM-GSt / Σt eHS-GSt )(39)

is far tighter than the difference of the two marginal intervals would suggest, because the shared channel realization cancels. Reporting overlapping marginal CIs and concluding "no difference" would be the wrong test here; the gain CI is the correct one, and it is the interval quoted whenever a claim about HS-GS vs EM-GS is made.

Note the intervals are on the Monte Carlo uncertainty only. They do not cover model misspecification, hyperparameter choice, or the fact that Lk is drawn from an assumed distribution.

12.4 Catastrophic trials — checked, and not what is driving the result

A pooled ratio of sums is vulnerable to a single bad trial. Two guards are computed at every point:

  • em_worst_share = the largest single trial's share of ΣeEM-GS. Across all 36 B3 points the maximum is 0.0361 (3.6%) and the minimum 0.0031. No point is dominated by one trial.
  • gain_trimmed95_db = the pooled gain recomputed after dropping the worst 5% of EM-GS trials. The largest discrepancy anywhere in B3 is 0.72 dB, at (N=16, P=10, SNR=20), where the pooled gain is −0.683 dB and the trimmed gain −1.403 dB — i.e. trimming makes HS-GS look worse, not better. At the headline point (N=32) trimming reduces the gain from 2.71 to 2.04 dB: the advantage survives trimming.

Conclusion: the reported gains are not outlier artefacts, and the trimming direction is adverse-to-HS-GS at every point where it matters, which is the honest direction for the check to fail in.

12.5 Win rate and the tie problem

win_rate_vs_em = fraction of trials with eHS-GSt < eEM-GSt. This looked alarming at N = 8 (33.5%) until the cause was identified: exact ties. When the order selector picks L̂ ≥ the rank cap, the Hankel projection is a no-op and HS-GS returns EM-GS's output bit for bit, so the strict inequality is false. The tie fraction matches the constraint-inactive fraction trial for trial (57.8% tied vs 42.2% active at N=8), which is the arithmetic identity one expects if — and only if — that explanation is right. Both tie_frac and a tie-free median gain are therefore stored alongside the raw win rate, and the raw win rate at N = 8 should never be quoted without the tie fraction beside it.

At N = 32 ties essentially vanish (0–0.25%) because the rank cap is 16 and L̂ ≈ 5, so the constraint is active in ≥ 99.75% of trials and the win rate of 95.3% is a genuine per-trial statistic.

12.6 Convergence failures verified

None were observed. Every estimator runs a fixed iteration budget (50) with no early stopping and no divergence test, so "convergence failure" here can only mean a non-finite output; the stores contain no NaN or Inf numerators across all 28,000 Track-B trials. This is a weaker statement than "the algorithms converged" — a fixed-budget iteration that has not converged still returns a finite number. The 50-iteration budget is inherited from Cui and was not tuned.

Figure B3 — new experiment, designed and run for this artifact

The specification calls for a figure of NMSE vs controlled path count L, to test the structural hypothesis directly. No such data existed in the Track-B stores: B3–B6 all draw Lk ~ 𝒰{3..7} at random, so nothing isolated the effect of L. Experiment B7 was therefore written and run — N = 32 (cap 16), P = 30, SNR = 5 dB, RSR = 12 dB, L ∈ {2,4,…,16} fixed and identical across users, 400 trials per point, 3,200 trials total, complete.

The hypothesis was recorded in the script before the run started, and is quoted verbatim from run_b7_pathcount.py:

"L increases → Hankel rank increases → the strict low-rank prior carries less information → the HS-GS advantage should shrink, and should vanish once L reaches cap(N) = ⌈N/2⌉, where the constraint is vacuous."

It was confirmed: the gain decays strictly monotonically from +7.13 dB at L = 2 to +0.04 dB at L = 14 (CI straddling zero) and −0.12 dB at the cap. The results, the refinement the data forces on the prediction, and why the null lands one grid point early are in §16.8. The figure is below.

13.4 Figures deliberately not produced

  • Error bars on the main NMSE curves. Excluded by standing instruction. The uncertainty is reported instead as explicit CI columns in the Part XVI tables and on the gain figure B1b, where the paired interval is the statistically meaningful one (§12.3).
  • Any figure containing the pre-correction CRLB. The originally shipped unconstrained bound had a real defect (§10.3) and disagreed with the corrected bound by up to 4.19 dB. It is described in the text but never plotted, per the instruction not to place an invalid bound on a graph.
  • Constellation / BER figures. Track B is a channel-estimation study. No detector is implemented, so any BER curve would be fabricated.
